# Authored spectroscopy lesson
This original fixture is an ideal illustration, not measured copper data or a PESU exam question.
Absorbance A is -log10(T), where transmittance T=I/I0 is a fraction, not a percentage. Within the Beer-Lambert linear range, A=epsilon*l*c. Epsilon is the molar absorption coefficient in L mol^-1 cm^-1, l is path length in cm, c is concentration in mol L^-1, and A is dimensionless. The incident and transmitted intensities must refer to the same wavelength and geometry. A dilute copper(II), Cu^2+, solution is an illustrative application; its coefficient depends on the chemical species and wavelength. Do not treat an illustrative coefficient as a universal constant for Cu^2+.
For an illustrative epsilon=100 L mol^-1 cm^-1, l=1 cm and c=0.002 mol L^-1, A=0.2. Then T=10^-0.2=0.6309573444801932, or 63.0957 percent. Do not insert 63.0957 in -log10(T); insert 0.630957. A linear A versus c calibration passes through the origin only for the ideal blank-corrected model; real intercepts must be measured, not fabricated.
