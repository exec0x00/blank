Unit 1, Introduction to the Constitution of India.


Constitution and Constitutional Law.

Reference material: Bare Act The Constitution of India (Universal's, 2020); M P Jain, Indian Constitutional Law; The Oxford Handbook of the Indian Constitution (Sujit Choudhry, Madhav Khosla, Pratap Bhanu Mehta). A Constitution shapes the aspirations of the country, the State's fundamental political views and its social values. Constitutional Law is a public law; it is the source of power and exercise of power in itself, and all laws of a country are structured within its limits. A Constitution is legal document(s) rooted in the socio-political history of a nation that also impacts its future. Kinds: written and unwritten; unitary and federal.

Written constitution ( for example India; USA We the People): detailed provisions, formal source of all constitutional powers, supreme or fundamental law, organs of the State are its creation, special amendment procedure, rigid. Unwritten constitution: combination of regular legislations plus conventions; the Parliament or Legislature is sovereign; for example United Kingdom, Magna Carta 1215, Provisions of Oxford 1258, Petition of Right 1628, Bill of Rights 1689.

Constitution and Constitutional Law are used interchangeably but differ: Constitutional Law means Constitution plus Relevant Legislations plus Constitutional Customs plus Relevant Precedents. Relevant laws means laws specifically mandated by the Constitution (Article 11, Article 17, Article 300). Constitutional customs or conventions means non-legal norms, not enforceable as of right, followed by stakeholders; non-compliance attracts public outcry, for example appointment of Prime Minister (Article 75) and Chief Justice of India (Article 124). Precedents: Judges Cases One to Four, UNR Rao versus Indira Gandhi (A.I.R. 1971 Supreme Court 1002), all Supreme Court or High Court decisions that clarify, restate or reiterate the constitutional position.


Constitutionalism.

Old concept, gained international traction in the last 3 to 4 decades. It is the idea that government can and should be legally limited in its powers and its authority or legitimacy depends on observing these limits (linked to Rule of Law and John Locke's Social Contract theory). It limits the government and maximises governance, for example written constitution, independent judiciary, judicial review, rule of law, separation of powers. A country can have a constitution but lack constitutionalism, and may have no constitution yet have constitutionalism. Elements listed: written constitution, supremacy of constitution, rigid amendment procedure, rule of law, separation of power, fundamental rights, judiciary, federalism, parliamentary government, universal adult suffrage.


Historical Background.

Constitution adopted 26 November 1949, came into force 26 January 1950; legal history traced to Government of India Acts 1919 and 1935. Timeline: East India Company, then 1857 First War of Independence, then 1858 direct rule of British Crown, then Indian Councils Act 1861 (Indians not in councils), then Indian National Congress 1885, then Indian Councils Act 1892 (some Indians included, but not in Executive Council), then Minto-Morley Reforms 1909 (more representation in legislature but non-elected members were more; none in executive council; no solution to representation), then World War One, Gandhi's return, trade union movement, then Montagu-Chelmsford Report, then Government of India Act 1919.

Government of India Act 1919: passed as a token of gratitude for India's role in World War One; increased native participation; created Diarchy (Chelmsford-Montagu reform); bicameral legislature at federal level; separate communal electorate; High Commissioner in London; to be reviewed after 10 years. Followed by Jallianwala Bagh, Bhagat Singh, Poorna Swaraj, Simon Commission (as per the slide: 1930). Government of India Act 1935: review of 1919 Act; abolished diarchy; Federation of India (didn't materialise); almost independent parliamentary government in provinces; federal executive, legislature, judiciary. Then World War Two; Indian National Congress did not participate in elections; Muslim League's Lahore Resolution. Cabinet Mission or Cripps Mission set up the Constituent Assembly.

Idea proposed in 1934 by M. N. Roy; August Offer and Cabinet Mission. 1946 election: 389 seats (292 provinces, 93 princely states, 4 Chief Commissioner provinces), united India. After Two-Nation theory and Indian Independence Act (18 July 1947), Constituent Assembly redrawn to 299 delegates; worked 114 days over 2 years 11 months 18 days. 9 December 1946 first meeting; 13 December 1946 Objective Resolution; 22 July 1947 National Flag adopted; 29 August 1947 Drafting Committee, Chairman Dr B. R. Ambedkar plus 6 members: K. M. Munshi, Muhammed Saadulah, Alladi Krishnaswamy Iyer, Gopala Swami Ayyangar, N. Madhava Rao, T. T. Krishnamachari.

Drafting: B. R. Ambedkar. Rules of Procedure: Rajendra Prasad. Union Power: J. Nehru. States (negotiating with states): J. Nehru. Union Constitution: J. Nehru. Steering: Rajendra Prasad. Provincial Constitution: V. Patel. Ad hoc Committee on National Flag: Rajendra Prasad. Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights, Minorities, Tribal and Excluded Areas: V. Patel. Functions of the Constituent Assembly: G. V. Mavlankar. Fundamental Rights Sub-Committee: J. B. Kripalani. House: G. Pattabhi Sitaramayya. Minorities Sub-Committee: Harendra Coomar Mukherjee. Language: Moturi Satyanarayana. North-East Frontier Tribal and Assam Excluded Areas: Gopinath Bordoloi. Order of Business: K. M. Munshi. Excluded Areas (other than Assam): A. V. Thakkar.


Salient Features (15).

Written constitution, Supremacy of Constitution, Rigid amendment, Rule of Law, Separation of Power, Parliamentary government, Single citizenship, Single judiciary, Secularism, Federalism, Universal Adult Suffrage, Fundamental Rights, Directive Principles of State Policy, Fundamental Duties, Preamble.

Written: 471 Articles, 25 Parts, 12 Schedules (originally 395 Articles, 8 Schedules); longest constitution in the world (United States: originally 7 articles, 27 added by amendments). Written means clear and unambiguous; detailed because of Centre-State division of power, responsibilities of organs, citizen-State relationship, enforcement of rights and duties, official language, citizenship, civil servants. Supremacy: United Kingdom, supremacy of Parliament; India, supremacy of Constitution; limits of powers or privileges set out clearly. Single citizenship plus single constitution, a criticism of India's federal nature. Single judiciary, three-tier unified judiciary: Supreme Court, then High Court, then clause a, Metropolitan courts: civil, City Civil Courts, Courts of Small Causes; criminal, Sessions Judge, Chief Metropolitan Court, Metropolitan Magistrate Courts; clause b, District and Sessions Court: civil, District Court, Sub Court, Principal Junior Civil Judge Court, Munsif Court; criminal, Sessions Court, Judicial Magistrate First Class, Judicial Magistrate Second Class.

Rigid amendment: stability; rigid yet flexible; amended 105 times in about 70 years yet procedure is difficult. Article 368: simple majority or special majority or special majority plus ratification by one half of States.

Separation of Powers (Legislature, Executive, Judiciary), three tenets: same person not in more than one organ; one organ must not interfere with another; one organ must not exercise another's functions. Strict, United States; looser, United Kingdom; India, unique model: division of powers, no strict separation. Legislature (law making) also does judicial functions (impeachment, contempt of legislature, nullifying judicial decisions) and executive functions (creating Supreme Court or High Court, deciding their jurisdiction and powers). Executive (law enforcing) also does legislative work (delegated legislation, ordinance, emergency) and judicial work (pardon, Article 72). Judiciary (dispute resolving) also makes rules (staff appointment, Article 145), judicial review, Article 142 (law until suitable legislation is made), and judicial review of executive action.

Rule of Law, 3 components: absence of arbitrary power, equality before law, independence of legal spirit protected by courts; propounded while comparing French and English legal systems. In India: judicial review (Article 13, 32, 226), independence of judiciary (Article 50), equality and freedom (Article 14 to 22), Preamble, democratic government.

Federalism: political organisation uniting separate states within an overarching system while each keeps its integrity. Federal features: written constitution, supremacy, division of power, rigidity, independent judiciary. Unique Indian federalism: Article 1 Union of States, single citizenship, single constituent authority, long Concurrent List, Parliament can legislate on State List, emergency. Problems of United States federalism: economic disparities, school-funding disparities, health-care access, conflicting judicial decisions, rights and responsibilities. SR Bommai versus Union of India (1994) 3 S.C.C. 1, federalism And secularism are basic structure.

Parliamentary form: President means nominal head; real executive means Cabinet with Prime Minister; tenure at will of legislature; collective responsibility. Presidential form: chief executive is real head, irremovable by legislature, legislature cannot dictate him, cabinet appointed from the people. Presidential benefits: stable, able ministers, President outside party politics (parliamentary may lead to authoritarianism). Parliamentary benefits: responsible; Prime Minister may be removed by Parliament anytime. India chose parliamentary because: familiar, harmony between legislature and executive, responsibility, assessment (daily and periodic).

Secularism: Western means separate religion and State; Indian means equal respect for all religions, not anti-religion or indifferent; word added by 42nd Amendment, but principle already in Article 25 to 28, 15 clause 1, 15 clause 2, 16 clause 2, Article 29 clause 2, 325; basic structure (SR Bommai). Universal Adult Suffrage: free and fair elections, Article 326; voting age 21, then 18 by 61st Amendment, 1988.

Fundamental Rights, Part 3, Article 12 to 35 (from United States Bill of Rights); Directive Principles of State Policy, Part 4, non-enforceable guiding principles (from Irish Constitution); Fundamental Duties, Part 4 A, added by 42nd Amendment on Swaran Singh Committee report (from Japanese Constitution).

Preamble: modified version of the Objective Resolution (13 December 1946). Tells who made the Constitution, its source, ultimate sanction, nature of polity, goals and objectives; three parts. Text: WE, The People OF India... Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic... Justice social, economic and political; Liberty of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship; Equality of status and opportunity; Fraternity assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation; IN Our Constituent Assembly this 26th November 1949... Adopt, Enact And Give TO Ourselves. Union of the trinity, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity. Preamble has no power, guiding principles only. Is it part of the Constitution?, In Re Berubari (A.I.R. 1960 Supreme Court 845) and Kesavananda Bharati.


Citizenship (Part 2, Article 5 to 11 plus Citizenship Act 1955).

Part 3 speaks of Persons (Article 14) and Citizens (Article 19). Citizenship governed by Part 2 (Article 5 to 11) and Citizenship Act, 1955 (Act 57 of 1955). Article 5, citizenship at commencement: domicile in India And clause a, born in India, or clause b, either parent born in India, or clause c, ordinarily resident at least 5 years before commencement. Domicile means residence plus intention to stay permanently; by origin (father's domicile at birth; posthumous child, English versus Indian law) or by choice. Article 6, migrants from Pakistan: he or parent or grandparent born in India (Government of India Act 1935) and clause i, migrated before 19 July 1948 and ordinarily resident since, or (ii) migrated on or after 19 July 1948 and registered by an officer of the Dominion of India (needs 6 months residence before application). Article 7, persons who migrated to Pakistan after 1 March 1947 are not citizens, unless they returned under a permit for resettlement (then treated as migrating after 19 July 1948). Article 8, persons of Indian origin residing outside India, registered by Indian diplomatic or consular representative. Article 9, voluntarily acquiring foreign citizenship, then not a citizen. Article 10, continuance of citizenship subject to Parliament's law. Article 11, Parliament to regulate citizenship by law (acquisition and termination). Laws listed: Citizenship Act (slide also says 1950), Amendment Acts 2003, 2005 (Overseas Citizenship), 2019. Modes: Birth, Descent, Registration, Naturalisation, Incorporation of territory.


State, Article 12.

State includes: clause a, Government and Parliament of India; clause b, Government and Legislature of each State; clause c, all local or other authorities within India or under control of the Government of India, unless the context otherwise requires. A statutory authority need not perform governmental or sovereign functions; State can engage in trade (Article 298, 46). Test includes power to give directions whose disobedience is punishable. Other authority means public administrative agency or corporation with quasi-governmental powers administering a revenue-producing public enterprise. Justice Shah (separate concurring opinion): other authorities means those invested by law with power to exercise sovereign powers.


Article 13 and its Doctrines.

Article 13 clause 1, pre-constitutional laws inconsistent with Part 3 are void to the extent of inconsistency (void from 26-01-1950). Article 13 clause 2, State shall not make law taking away or abridging Fundamental Rights; post-constitutional laws are void ab initio. Article 13 clause 3clause a,, law includes Ordinance, order, bye-law, rule, regulation, notification, custom or usage having force of law (order, under statutory authority; bye-law, local authorities; rule, delegated legislation; regulation, day-to-day; notification, announcement). Article 13 clause 3clause b,, laws in force means laws made before commencement and not repealed. Article 13 clause 4, Article 13 does not apply to amendments under Article 368.

Three doctrines: Doctrine of Eclipse, a pre-constitutional law inconsistent with Fundamental Rights is not wiped out; it stays for rights or liabilities accrued before commencement and is eclipsed or dormant by the Fundamental Rights. Doctrine of Severability, law void only to the extent of inconsistency; if the invalid part is severable, the rest stands; if not separable, whole statute goes; intention of the legislature is the determining factor (a general principle of interpretation). Waiver of Fundamental Rights, no person can waive Fundamental Rights, even voluntarily.

Amendment cases: Shankari Prasad versus Union of India, amendment is not law under 13 clause 2; Sajjan Singh versus State of Rajasthan, followed it; I.C. Golaknath versus State of Punjab, overruled both; 24th Amendment, 1971; Kesavananda Bharati versus State of Kerala, Basic Structure.


Right to Equality, Article 14 to 18.

Article 14 to 18 (concentric: 14 contains 15 contains 16 contains 17 contains 18); specifically mentioned in Preamble. Article 14: State shall not deny to any person equality before law (British, negative concept, Rule of Law) or equal protection of laws (United States, positive concept; not absolute equality, equals treated equally, unequals not equally). Article 14 forbids class legislation, not reasonable classification. Test: (1) intelligible differentia, not arbitrary, artificial or evasive; real and substantial distinction; (2) rational nexus with the object of the Act ( for example Sec 11, Indian Contract Act); object must be lawful.

Article 15 and 16 apply only to citizens. Article 15 clause 1, no discrimination on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth. Article 15 clause 2, no disability re clause a, shops, public restaurants, hotels, places of public entertainment; clause b, wells, tanks, bathing ghats, roads, places of public resort maintained by State funds or dedicated to public. Article 15 clause 3, special provisions for women and children (job reservation for women permissible even without Article 16 provision). Article 15 clause 4, special provisions for socially and educationally backward classes, Supreme Court, Scheduled Tribes (also overrides Article 29 clause 2). Article 15 clause 5, special provisions for socially and educationally backward classes or Supreme Court or Scheduled Tribes admission to educational institutions including private (aided or unaided), except minority institutions under Article 30 clause 1.

Article 16 clause 1, equality of opportunity in public employment: right to be considered, no right to be appointed; covers matters prior and subsequent to employment, termination or removal, under the State. Article 16 clause 2, no discrimination on religion, race, caste, sex, descent, place of birth, residence (16 clause 1 is wider; no restriction on private persons). Gazula Dasaratha Rama Rao versus State of AP, Madras Hereditary Village Officers Act, 1895 (descent); compassionate appointments? 16 clause 3, Parliament may prescribe residence requirement. Article 17, Untouchability abolished, practice in any form forbidden and punishable; Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955. Article 18, abolition of titles (Rai Bahadur, Diwan and so on.).


Freedoms, Article 19.

Natural rights protected by the State; only citizens; not absolute, reasonable restrictions only by authority of law (not executive order), related to purposes in 19 clause 2-(6). Article 19 clause 1clause a, speech and expression (words, writing, printing, picture, film, painting; also parliamentary privileges issue), clause b, assembly, clause c, association, clause d, movement, clause e, residence, clause g, profession, occupation, trade or business. Article 19 clause 2, 8 grounds: sovereignty and integrity of India, security of State, friendly relations with foreign States, public order, decency or morality, contempt of court, defamation, incitement to an offence.

Shreya Singhal versus Union of India: Sec 66A, Information Technology Act 2000 (punished e-messages causing annoyance or inconvenience or misleading) struck down, grounds not proximate to any of the 8 subjects in 19 clause 2. Article 19 clause 6: restrictions on 19 clause 1clause g, in interest of general public, including clause i, professional or technical qualifications and (ii) State trade or monopoly to exclusion of citizens (1st Amendment, 1951). Betting and gambling is not trade, game of chance not skill. Slaughter of animals, butchers' trade versus general public interest; initially economic approach.


Article 20 to 24.

Article 20 clause 1, ex post facto law: conviction only under law in force at the time; no greater penalty than then. Article 20 clause 2, double jeopardy: not prosecuted and punished twice. Article 20 clause 3, self-incrimination: accused not compelled to be witness against himself. Forensic techniques (narco-analysis, polygraph, B.E.O.S. profiling, DNA, voice spectrography, biometrics): for, aids truth-finding, corroboration, conviction rates; against, may violate Article 20 clause 3 and 21, coercive, misuse or human-rights concerns.

Article 21, no person deprived of life and personal liberty except by procedure established by law. Life extends to all limbs and faculties (Bandhua Mukti Morcha versus Union of India). A.K. Gopalan versus State of Madras (A.I.R. 1950 Supreme Court 27), Article 21 covers procedural law or executive action, not legislative. Maneka Gandhi versus Union of India (1978), procedure must not be unfair or unreasonable; covers legislative action too. Conditions: valid law plus procedure plus just, fair and reasonable plus satisfies Article 14 and 19. Life includes environment, livelihood, health, dignity, privacy, education, shelter, sleep; virginity test and telephone tapping (privacy). Digital Constitutionalism means applying constitutional values to the digital environment (internet governance, platform regulation, AI ethics, surveillance, data privacy). Surveillance laws regulate collection, storage, use of personal data by State (email, phone, Cctv) to protect against terrorism, espionage, cyber-attacks, but raise privacy concerns.

Article 22: (1) told grounds of arrest plus consult legal practitioner of choice; (2) produced before nearest magistrate within 24 hours (excluding journey); (3) (1) and (2) don't apply to enemy aliens or preventive detention; (4) preventive detention maximum 3 months unless Advisory Board (persons qualified as High Court Judges) reports sufficient cause; (5) grounds communicated plus earliest opportunity of representation; (6) facts against public interest need not be disclosed; (7) Parliament may prescribe cases of detention beyond 3 months without Board, maximum period, Board procedure. Preventive detention re defence, foreign affairs, security, by Central Government. Article 23 to 24, Right against Exploitation: 23 bans trafficking, begar and forced labour (offence; Bandhua Mukti Morcha); 24, no child below 14 in factory, mine or hazardous employment.


Religion and Minorities, Article 25 to 30.

Article 25 clause 1, all persons: freedom of conscience and to profess, practise, propagate religion, subject to public order, morality, health. Article 25 clause 2clause a,, State may regulate economic, financial, political, secular activity associated with religion (Jehovah's Witnesses opposing war, national security). Article 25 clause 2clause b,, social welfare or reform and throwing open Hindu religious institutions of public character to all Hindus. Article 26, religious denominations: clause a, establish institutions for religious or charitable purposes, clause b, manage own religious affairs, clause c, own or acquire property, clause d, administer it per law. Article 27, no compulsion to pay taxes (not fees) specifically appropriated for promoting any religion. Article 28: (1) no religious instruction in institutions wholly maintained by State funds; (2) exception, State-administered but established under endowment or trust requiring it; (3) State-recognised or aided, may have it but no compulsion without consent (guardian if minor).

Article 29 clause 1, any section of citizens with distinct language, script or culture may conserve it. Article 29 clause 2, no denial of admission to State-maintained or aided institutions on religion, race, caste, language. Article 30 clause 1, minorities (religious or linguistic) may establish and administer educational institutions of their choice (read together, can administer if established; no unusual government control). Article 30 clause 1A, compensation on acquisition must not abrogate the right. Article 30 clause 2, no discrimination in grant-in-aid on minority management.


Article 32, Constitutional Remedies.

Article 32 clause 1 right to move Supreme Court for Fundamental Rights is guaranteed; Article 32 clause 2 Supreme Court may issue writs, Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Quo Warranto, Certiorari; Article 32 clause 3 Parliament may empower other courts; Article 32 clause 4 not suspended except as provided by the Constitution.


Directive Principles of State Policy, Part 4 (Article 36 to 51).

Move from laissez faire to welfare state; no political democracy without economic democracy; borrowed from Irish Constitution. Article 36, State; Article 37, not enforceable by any court, but fundamental in governance; duty of State to apply them in making laws. Reasons: positive obligations, financial crunch, framers never intended enforcement; no mandamus to implement Directive Principles of State Policy; courts should interpret to further Directive Principles of State Policy goals. Conflict with Fundamental Rights: initially strict, State of Madras versus Champakam Dorairajan (communal quota order); Directive Principles of State Policy subsidiary. Ambedkar: Constitution lays down political democracy plus ideals of economic democracy. Nehru: Fundamental Rights static, Directive Principles of State Policy dynamic. Golaknath, both integral; Kesavananda Bharati, Fundamental Rights case. Article 31C, laws giving effect to Directive Principles of State Policy not void for violating Article 14 or 19; State laws need President's assent.

Article 38, social order with justice (social, economic, political); minimise inequalities in income, status, facilities (taxation also reduces inequality; linked with 19 clause 1clause d,, 21). Article 39, clause a, livelihood (with 21; N.R.E.G.A. 2005), clause b, and clause c, distributive economic system or socialistic (nationalisation, taking over sick industries), clause d, equal pay for equal work (Equal Remuneration Act 1976; Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act; Juvenile Justice Act). 39A, free legal aid (Article 21; Kerala Court Fees Act legal service fund; Legal Services Authorities Act). Article 40, village panchayats (grassroots democracy, Panchayat Raj Act). 41, right to work, education, public assistance (unemployment, old age, sickness, disablement). 42, humane conditions of work (Factories Act, Maternity Benefit Act). 43, living wage. 43A, workers' participation in management. 43B, co-operative societies. 44, Uniform Civil Code. 46, interests of Supreme Court or Scheduled Tribes.


Right to Property and Fundamental Duties.

44th Amendment, 1978 abolished right to property as an Fundamental Rights; before 1978 it was in Article 19 clause 1clause f, (acquire, hold, dispose) and Article 31; natural law, United States 5th Amendment; India's socialist impact, abolition of zamindari, slum clearance, nationalisation. Eminent domain means private property for public use without owner's consent, with just compensation. Article 19 clause 5, public interest and Scheduled Tribes.

Fundamental Duties (Article 51A) of every citizen: clause a, abide by Constitution, respect its ideals, institutions, National Flag and Anthem; clause b, follow ideals of freedom struggle; clause c, uphold sovereignty, unity, integrity; clause d, defend country, national service; clause e, promote harmony and brotherhood; renounce practices derogatory to dignity of women; clause f, preserve composite culture; clause g, protect environment (forests, lakes, rivers, wildlife), compassion for living creatures; clause i, safeguard public property, abjure violence; clause j, strive for excellence; clause k, parent or guardian to provide education to child aged 6 to 14. (clause h, is not on the slides.)


Unit 2, Federal System: Legislature, Executive and Judiciary.


President (Part 5, Article 52 to 78).

Union Executive means President, Vice-President, Council of Ministers headed by Prime Minister, and Attorney General; parliamentary form, close to British model. Article 52, there shall be a President; Article 53, executive power vested in President, exercised directly or through subordinate officers per Constitution. Article 54, elected by electoral college of elected members of both Houses of Parliament plus elected members of State Legislative Assemblies (including Delhi and Puducherry); nominated members and Legislative Council members cannot vote. Article 62, election before term ends; vacancy (death, resignation, removal) filled within 6 months; new President gets a full 5-year term. Article 56, term 5 years; resigns in writing to Vice-President (who informs the Speaker of Lok Sabha); removed by impeachment for violation of Constitution.

Article 58, qualifications: citizen; at least 35 years; qualified to be Lok Sabha member (registered voter); no office of profit (President, Vice-President, Governor, Union or State Minister are not offices of profit); not an MP. Re-election allowed without restriction. Article 59: not a member of any House (seat deemed vacated on taking office); no office of profit; rent-free residence; emoluments fixed by Parliament and cannot be reduced during term; free medical facilities, treatment for life, state car; first citizen. Salary: 1951 10,000 rupees ( plus 15,000 allowance); 1985 15,000 rupees ( plus 30,000); 1989 20,000 rupees ( plus 50,000); 1998 50,000 rupees; 2008 1,50,000 rupees; 2016 5,00,000 rupees; 2024 about 5 rupees.9 lakh. Rashtrapati Bhavan, largest residence of any head of state. Article 60, oath before the Chief Justice of India (or senior-most Supreme Court judge): preserve, protect and defend the Constitution and the law.

Article 61, impeachment for violation of Constitution: charges by either House; resolution signed by at least one-fourth members, 14 days' notice, passed by two-thirds majority; other House investigates (President may appear or be represented); if it too passes by two-thirds, President removed from that date. Article 361, immunity: not answerable to any court for official acts (but conduct may be reviewed by a body appointed by Parliament for impeachment; suits against Government of India not barred); no criminal proceedings and no arrest or imprisonment process during term; civil proceedings for personal acts only after 2 months' written notice stating nature, cause, party details and relief.

Powers, Executive: appoints Prime Minister and Council of Ministers, Supreme Court or High Court judges, Governors, Attorney General, Comptroller and Auditor General, Chief Election Commissioner and members; administers Union Territories (Chief Commissioners, Lt Governors); removes ministers, Governors, Attorney General; right to be informed. Military: Supreme Commander of armed forces; appoints chiefs; war and peace in his name. Diplomatic: ambassadors are his representatives; receives credentials; negotiates treaties before Parliament's ratification. Legislative: first to address Parliament in budget session; summons joint session to break deadlock; sanction needed for new State or boundary or name changes, Fundamental Rights-related legislation, money bills in Lok Sabha; assent needed for all bills; ordinances; nominates members. Financial: Finance Commission reports, Contingency Fund at his disposal, audit reports laid. Judicial: rectify judicial errors, pardons, seek Supreme Court advice (legal, constitutional, national importance).

Article 123, Ordinance: when neither House is in session and immediate action needed; same effect as an Act; must be laid before both Houses; ceases 6 weeks after reassembly; may expire if both Houses disapprove; withdrawable anytime; void if unconstitutional. Article 72, Pardon in clause i, court-martial, (ii) offences against Union-matter laws, (iii) death sentence. Pardon, fully absolves (as if never committed); Reprieve, temporary suspension of death sentence (pending pardon or commutation); Respite, lesser punishment on special grounds ( for example pregnancy); Remission, reduce quantity without changing character (1 years, then 6 months); Commutation, substitute lighter form (rigorous, then simple imprisonment). Emergency powers (slide under Article 352): national emergency (external aggression or armed rebellion, on recommendation of Prime Minister and Council); State breakdown (Governor's rule); financial instability (control expenditure).


Vice-President (Article 63 to 71).

Article 63; ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha; acts as President on death or removal or resignation, or discharges functions during illness or absence, then gets President's salary and ceases to be Rajya Sabha Chairman. Acting period until new President: maximum 6 months. If both offices vacant: Chief Justice of India (or senior-most Supreme Court judge) acts, President (Discharge of Functions) Act 1969 under Article 70. Functions (Article 64, 65): casting vote in tie in Rajya Sabha, represents Rajya Sabha ceremonially, protects members' privileges, goodwill missions abroad. Qualifications: citizen, 35 years, not an MP, qualified for Rajya Sabha, no office of profit [Article 66 clause 4]; nomination by 20 proposers plus 20 seconders. Election (Article 66): electoral college of all Members of Parliament of both Houses, proportional representation by single transferable vote, secret ballot; disputes, Supreme Court under Article 71 clause 1; acts done before election declared void remain valid; vacancies in college no ground to invalidate; election before term ends [Article 68 clause 1], immediately on vacancy [Article 68 clause 2]. Oath before President (Article 69); no office of profit (Article 64); MP or MLA seat deemed vacated [Article 66 clause 2]; allowances, Second Schedule (Article 97). Tenure (Article 67): 5 years, continues until successor; resigns to President; removal: Rajya Sabha resolution by absolute majority plus Lok Sabha agreement by simple majority, 14 days' notice, no inquiry needed.


Council of Ministers (Article 74 to 75).

Article 74 clause 1, Council of Ministers headed by Prime Minister to aid and advise President; President may ask for reconsideration once, then bound by advice. Article 74 clause 2, advice tendered cannot be inquired into by any court. 91st Amendment 2003: Council of Ministers size at most 15 percent of Lok Sabha or Assembly strength (small States like Sikkim, Mizoram, Goa with less than 40 members excepted; States minimum 12 ministers including Chief Minister; excess reduced within 6 months). Three categories: Cabinet Ministers (heads of departments, attend cabinet as of right, can be without portfolio; constitutional status by 44th Amendment 1978); Ministers of State (independent charge, attend cabinet only when invited); Deputy Ministers (assist, no separate charge, lower salary, no part in cabinet).

Anti-defection (Article 102 clause 2, Article 191 clause 2, Tenth Schedule): disqualified if he voluntarily gives up party membership or votes or abstains contrary to party direction (unless condoned within 15 days); a split group of at least one-third was protected (slide on Article 164 clause 1B also says one-third defection ends membership and bars ministership). Article 75: (1) Prime Minister appointed by President; others on Prime Minister's advice; (1A) at most 15 percent of Lok Sabha; (1B) disqualified defector cannot be Minister till term ends or re-elected; (2) hold office during pleasure of President; (3) collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha; (4) oaths of office and secrecy (Third Schedule); (5) non-member ceases after 6 consecutive months; (6) salaries by Parliament or Second Schedule. Collective responsibility: every minister stands by Cabinet decisions or resigns; no-confidence vote against Prime Minister means against entire Council of Ministers. Individual responsibility to President [Article 75 clause 2], in practice Prime Minister asks minister to resign. Dissolution: Lok Sabha term 5 years [Article 83 clause 2]; President may dissolve earlier on Prime Minister's advice [85clause b,] or if no viable government. J. Jayalalitha case: person convicted and sentenced to at least 2 years cannot be appointed Chief Minister under Article 164 clause 1.


State Executive, Governor, Chief Minister (Article 153 to 167).

State executive means Governor, Chief Minister, Council of Ministers, Advocate-General; same parliamentary pattern. Article 153, Governor for each State; executive power vested in him (officers subordinate includes Ministers). Appointed by President; one person may be Governor of two or more States. Qualifications: citizen, 35 years, not from that State, Chief Minister consulted. Not a member of any legislature; no office of profit (Article 158); emoluments not reduced [Article 158 clause 4]; oath before Chief Justice of the High Court (Article 159). Article 156: holds office during pleasure of the President; resigns to President; 5-year term; continues till successor. Appoints Chief Minister and ministers (on Chief Minister's advice), Advocate-General, State Public Service Commission members, State Election Commission and State Finance Commission (Article 243K on slide).

Chief Minister: leader of majority party; if no majority, Governor's discretion; minimum age 25; oath before Governor. Article 163 clause 1, Council of Ministers with Chief Minister to aid and advise Governor, except where he acts in his discretion (President has no such discretion). Governor's discretion: appointing Chief Minister, dismissing minister, dissolving Assembly, advising President on Article 356. 91st Amendment added Article 164 clause 1A (15 percent cap, minimum 12) and Article 164 clause 1B (defection). Chief Minister powers: link between Cabinet and Governor; forms ministry (Governor cannot insist on names); allocates or reshuffles portfolios; presides over Council of Ministers; can seek dismissal or resignation of ministers; if Chief Minister resigns, government collapses; announces policies; advises summoning of sessions; Chairman of State Planning Board; member of Inter-State Council and National Development Council (headed by Prime Minister); Vice-chairman of Zonal Council by rotation; crisis manager in emergencies.


Parliament and State Legislature.

Article 79: Parliament means President plus Rajya Sabha (Council of States) plus Lok Sabha (House of the People); State legislature means Vidhana Sabha plus Vidhana Parishad plus Governor. India is a parliamentary republic with bicameralism; follows the USA pattern (both houses elected) rather than United Kingdom (House of Lords, nobility or peerage, inherited or for life; House of Commons, elected). Article 170, Assembly 60 to 500 members, direct election; Article 172, 5 years. Article 171, Council at most one-third of Assembly (elected from district boards, municipalities, local authorities). Article 173, citizen; Assembly 25 years, Council 30 years. Disqualifications: office of profit, unsound mind, undischarged insolvent (as per the slide: discharged insolvent), disqualified by Parliament's law.


Union Judiciary (Article 124 to 147).

Preamble places Justice (social, economic, political) above Liberty, Equality, Fraternity, and social and economic justice over political. Judiciary, an arm of the social revolution. Supreme Court means Chief Justice of India plus not more than 7 judges originally (now 30), appointed by President by warrant after consultation; hold office till 65 [Article 124 clause 2]; resign to President. Qualifications: citizen and 5 years High Court judge, or 10 years High Court advocate, or distinguished jurist (implied: integrity, impartiality, independence). Article 124 clause 4, removal: President's order after address by each House supported by majority plus two-thirds of present and voting, on proved misbehaviour or incapacity. Impeachment attempted 4 times: V. Ramaswami J, first (1993, Lok Sabha, failed two-thirds); Soumitra Sen J (Calcutta High Court), resigned 2011 after Rajya Sabha passed motion (first impeached by Upper House); J.B. Pardiwala J (2015, 58 Rajya Sabha Members of Parliament, reservation remarks); C.V. Nagarjuna Reddy J (2017); Dipak Misra Chief Justice of India (2018 draft).

Jurisdiction: Article 32 (Fundamental Rights enforcement, writs); Article 129, Court of Record (determine own jurisdiction, punish for contempt); Article 131, original and exclusive: Government of India versus State(s), Government of India plus States versus States, State versus State; Article 132, appeal if High Court certifies substantial question re Constitution; Article 133, civil (substantial question of law); Article 134, criminal: High Court reversed acquittal and sentenced to death, withdrew case and sentenced to death, or certifies fit case; Article 136, Special Leave to Appeal (discretion, any court or tribunal); Article 137, Review (new evidence, error apparent on record, other sufficient reason); Article 141, Supreme Court law binding on all courts; Article 143, Advisory jurisdiction on President's reference.


State Judiciary (High Courts).

High Court is highest court of the State; one High Court can serve two or more States; number of judges fixed by President. Chief Justice appointed by President in consultation with Chief Justice of India and Governor; other judges by President, Governor and High Court CJ. Writ powers (Article 226), habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, quo warranto, certiorari, for Fundamental Rights or for other purpose; superintendence over all courts and tribunals; consulted in appointment of District Judges; controls subordinate judiciary; original and appellate jurisdiction (Civil Procedure Code, Criminal Procedure Code, Letters Patent). Qualifications: citizen plus 10 years judicial office or 10 years High Court advocate. Retires at 62; resigns to President; after retirement may practise only in Supreme Court or High Courts where he did not serve. Article 219, oath before Governor. Removal: motion by 100 Lok Sabha members or 50 Rajya Sabha members; Speaker or Chairman may reject or form 3-member committee; if guilty, both Houses pass by special majority, then President removes.


Emergency (Article 352 to 360).

Novel feature: peace-time federalism adapts to emergency (Centre gets overriding power); aim, welfare state, greatest happiness of greatest number. Three types: National (352), war, external aggression or armed rebellion; only on written recommendation of Union Cabinet headed by Prime Minister; must be approved by Parliament within 1 month; proclaimed 1962 (China), 1971 (Indo-Pak), 1975 (internal). State or President's Rule (356 and 365), on Governor's report or otherwise; President may assume State functions, give legislative powers to Parliament; approval within 2 months; slide: State may suspend Article 19 rights during emergency. Financial (360), threat to financial stability or credit; Centre may direct canons of financial propriety and reduction of salaries; 44th Amendment made 360 self-contained: ceases after 2 months unless approved by both Houses; if Lok Sabha dissolved (Rajya Sabha approved), ceases 30 days after new Lok Sabha first sits unless approved.


Amendment and Basic Structure.

Article 368: bill in either House; passed in each by majority of total membership plus two-thirds of present and voting; then President's assent; certain provisions need ratification by at least one half of States (resolution of State legislatures). Basic Structure means fundamental principles forming the backbone of the Constitution. Shankari Prasad versus Union of India (A.I.R. 1951 Supreme Court 455), 5 judges: Article 368 gives Parliament power to amend Fundamental Rights. Golaknath versus State of Punjab (1967 A.I.R. 1643), 1st, 4th, 17th Amendments challenged; 11 judges, 6:5, Parliament cannot amend Part 3. Kesavananda Bharati (1973) 4 S.C.C. 225, first laid down Basic Structure; overruled Golaknath; Parliament cannot alter basic structure; Preamble is basic structure. 42nd Amendment added Socialist and Secular and clauses (4) and (5) to Article 368 (after Kesavananda and Raj Narain). Basic structure elements: constitutional supremacy, rule of law, judicial review, separation of powers, federalism, secularism, Fundamental Rights; acts as a legal shield, a most potent tool for checks and balances; debate continues on its scope and on applying it to ordinary laws. Article 370 (special status to J and K) abrogated 2019 by Presidential Orders and Parliamentary Resolutions; whole Constitution extended to J and K; challenged as violating federalism; Supreme Court (2023) upheld, Indian federalism is asymmetric.


Book 1, The Story of My Experiments with Truth (M. K. Gandhi).


The book.

Author Mahatma Gandhi; originally written in Gujarati as Satya Na Prayogo; translated into English by Mahadev Desai (also his personal secretary for many years); first part published 1925; first serialised in Navjivan. Main theme: spiritual and moral development; Gandhi saw his life as a series of Experiments with Truth. Gandhi: An Autobiography is a translation of this book. It covers his life up to 1921 (another question says it ends with 1920 because his experiments with truth were largely over).


Family and early life.

Born at Porbandar; father Karamchand Gandhi, a Diwan (Chief Minister); mother Putlibai; family intended him to become a Diwan like his father. Married Kasturba (called Ba) in 1883 at age 13; youngest son Devdas. Elder brother Laxmidas helped him go to London; mother made him vow not to touch wine or meat. Studied law at University of London; contracted pleurisy there, leading to diet experiments; influenced in London by Annie Besant. Ideal of truthfulness: Raja Harishchandra. First practised law in Bombay but failed due to shyness and inability to speak in court; briefly a teacher in Rajkot.


South Africa.

Went to South Africa in 1893 for a legal case with Dada Abdulla and Co., mostly for the Indian merchant community; stayed about 21 years. First racial discrimination at Pietermaritzburg, thrown off a train, sparking his first civil disobedience. Founded Phoenix Settlement (first settlement) and started weekly Indian Opinion; Hermann Kallenbach shared his spiritual and dietary experiments and was a key figure at Tolstoy Farm. Protest called Satyagraha ( means Truth force). Organised an Indian Ambulance corps in the Boer War; Black Act protest against law requiring Indians to carry registration certificates; first jail in South Africa, 1908 for refusing to register; Great March 1913 for Indian indentured labourers. Cleaned his own toilet after discrimination against a visitor in South Africa. Influences: Ruskin's Unto This Last (economic views), Tolstoy's The Kingdom of God is Within You, Thoreau (civil disobedience).


Return to India and movements.

Returned 1915; political guru Gopal Krishna Gokhale; received Kaiser-i-Hind medal in 1915, returned it after Jallianwala Bagh (1919, General Dyer). Sabarmati Ashram, Ahmedabad, 1917; Sevagram in Wardha, Maharashtra. First major movement: Champaran Satyagraha (1917), brought by Rajkumar Shukla; led to Champaran Agrarian Act benefiting indigo farmers. Kheda: peasants' tax relief due to famine. First major fast in India 1918 for Ahmedabad mill workers. Opposed Rowlatt Act (imprison without trial); supported Khilafat to promote Hindu-Muslim unity. Adopted dhoti in 1921. Suspended Non-Cooperation (1922) after Chauri Chaura. 21-day fast 1924 for Hindu-Muslim unity. Simon Commission boycotted, no Indian members. Bardoli Satyagraha 1928 led by Vallabhbhai Patel. Dandi March 1930 (salt tax), about 240 miles, started in Gujarat; British Prime Minister then Ramsay MacDonald. Gandhi-Irwin Pact 1931. 1932 fast against Communal Award, then Poona Pact with B. R. Ambedkar. Rejected August Offer 1940 (no immediate independence); Cripps Mission 1942 failed (no full independence); Cabinet Mission 1946 initially accepted by Gandhi. Quit India 1942, slogan Do or Die; jailed in Aga Khan Palace, where Kasturba (1944) and Mahadev Desai died; palace later donated in memory of Mahatma Gandhi. On 15 August 1947 he was in Calcutta. Assassinated 30 January 1948 at Birla House, Delhi; last words Hey Ram; cremated at Raj Ghat.


Ideas, titles and terms.

Title Mahatma ( means Great Soul) given by Tagore. Fundamental principle: Truth; essence of religion: Truth and Non-violence; all religions are equal paths to truth; means as important as ends. Ahimsa means non-violence (an eye for an eye makes the whole world blind); Swaraj means self-rule; Sarvodaya means welfare for all; Swadeshi means home-grown industries and goods; Aparigraha means non-possession; Asteya means non-stealing; Trusteeship means wealth distribution; Ram Rajya means utopian state of truth and justice; Talisman means think of the poorest and weakest. One of 11 ashram vows: Fearlessness. Silent Mondays, spiritual rest and conserving energy. Diet: mango led to fruit-diet belief; tried giving up salt; preferred nature cure (skeptical of modern medicine). Education: Basic Education (Nai Talim), craft alongside academics. Revived Khadi (hand-spinning and weaving); Charkha means economic self-sufficiency; Constructive Programme means social reform and self-reliance; villages, self-sufficiency and decentralisation; critical of industrialisation, favoured small-scale village industries. Hindi (Hindustani) as unifying language. Wrote Hind Swaraj (in Gujarati, on a voyage); started Navjivan (Gujarati) and Harijan (cause of Dalits). Vaikom Satyagraha means temple entry for Dalits. Philosophy means holistic approach to life; turned Indian National Congress into a mass movement; women's rights progressive for his time; conflict resolution by dialogue and non-violent pressure; favourite song Raghupati Raghava Raja Ram; Nehru, fatherly mentor and friend; wrote to Hitler to plead for peace; never received the Nobel Peace Prize; influenced Martin Luther King Jr.; Abdul Ghaffar Khan means Frontier Gandhi. 2 October means International Day of Non-Violence, declared by United Nations in 2007.


Book 2, My India: The India Eternal (Swami Vivekananda).


The book and core message.

Published by Ramakrishna Mission Institute of Culture. Soul of India means Spirituality; India's decline caused by loss of spiritual values; regeneration founded on spiritual awakening; value to revive for national pride means spirituality; India's future lies in spiritual awakening and service, guided by spirituality. India's destiny or mission: spiritual guide for humanity, India shall guide the world spiritually, offer spiritual leadership, share spiritual insights, tolerance and inner strength; it is India's duty to share the message. India's spiritual contribution or values are universal and eternal; heritage is India's greatest gift to the world; ancient civilisation spiritually advanced; ancient culture means foundation of India's greatness; scriptures contain eternal truths; Nalanda and so on. were centres of spiritual and intellectual excellence. Unity lies in spiritual values; spiritual unity means foundation of national strength; unity is essential for national strength. Villages means heart of India. River revered: Ganga. Festival of awakening: Durga Puja. Scripture quoted for national pride, moral strength (epic) and action: Bhagavad Gita. Western thinker admired: Emerson. Metaphor for India's dormant spiritual power: a sleeping lion (also in Stirring Utterances: sleeping lion that must awaken).


Strength, fear, faith.

Strength is life, weakness is death; greatest sin means weakness (The greatest sin is to think yourself weak, then undervaluing one's own potential). Strength (primary need) means physical, moral and spiritual vigour; strength needed for regeneration and to overcome adversity; physical strength essential for national regeneration. Fear must be conquered (for spiritual growth and to build the nation); quality most lacking under colonial rule: Courage; courage means foundation of strength; fearlessness, then moral courage, independence of mind, readiness to act for truth and service. Faith in oneself means foundation of strength; faith in oneself and the nation essential; self-confidence means key to national awakening; self-reliance means key to national regeneration; main obstacle to progress means lack of faith in one's own self. Thought shapes destiny; action means expression of (spiritual) strength. Spiritual knowledge must be accompanied by humility. Ideal youth: Strong, fearless, and full of faith.


Youth, women, education.

Key to India's future: Youth, builders of the future, agents of social transformation; message: be proud of your heritage and serve the nation. Women must be educated and empowered; women's education vital for national progress. Education must build character and self-confidence; character means true goal or foundation of education; education builds character, confidence and usefulness to society. Education is the manifestation of perfection already in man, then draws out inner potential and divinity. Teacher should inspire independence and courage; criticised education giving superficial knowledge without character; colonial education ignored India's soul.


Service, religion, society.

Uplift nation by serving the poor and downtrodden; best way to serve God means serving humanity; service to poor means highest form of worship; service means highest form of religion; temples and rituals are secondary to service; monks should serve humanity or society. Poverty means national shame, a moral and social problem needing education, uplift and practical measures. Caste discrimination condemned as divisive; caste a social evil when rigidly hereditary, duties and merits matter, not birth. Blind ritualism, then superstition; superstition must be eradicated; blind faith, then replaced by reason and experience. Religious tolerance essential for unity; Dharma means foundation of tolerance; Tolerance means key to universal brotherhood; Universal acceptance can unite the world ( means recognising unity behind diverse religions while critiquing harmful practices); pluralism: pluralistic, non-exclusive. Conversion weakens national identity. Religion should guide moral and social conduct. Social reform guided by Compassion. Spiritual teachers or saints means true leaders, true heroes, torchbearers of India's soul. National pride essential for progress. Indians abroad should represent India's spiritual heritage.


India and the West; philosophy.

Visited the United States. Warned against blind imitation of the West (it weakens India's identity); Learn everything that is good from others, but do not lose your own identity, then absorb useful knowledge, preserve individuality; learn the West's organisation, science, industry while keeping spiritual strengths. West's materialism must be balanced with India's spirituality; critique of West means spiritual emptiness despite material power. Value misunderstood by West: Renunciation; renunciation means renouncing selfishness plus active service. Material progress valuable if subordinated to moral or spiritual ends; national progress needs moral, spiritual, intellectual uplift of masses alongside material. Answer to missionaries: strengthen own spiritual and social institutions. Science and religion: complementary. Atman is Brahman means identity of individual Self with ultimate Reality (non-dual); Upanishadic basis of service means divinity of the Self, same Self in others. Practical Vedanta, then social welfare, education, human dignity. Style: emotional patriotism plus philosophical reasoning; lofty metaphysics plus concrete calls for reform and education. National integration means unity of thought, character and purpose; Strengthen the mind of the nation..., then strong, virtuous, self-reliant citizens.


Life events.

Spiritual master: Ramakrishna Paramahamsa; convinced Ramakrishna had realised God by the divine ecstasy and sincerity in his reply. Father's death brought poverty. After Ramakrishna's passing disciples formed brotherhood at Baranagar Math. Vowed to travel barefoot and know the real India; wandered to know India and experience its people's lives; saw masses suffering poverty, ignorance, lack of education; touched by villagers' devotion and simplicity despite poverty. Alwar taught: true religion means serving people, not rituals. Meditated on Kanyakumari Rock before going West, saw the vast ocean and rocky island; insight: regeneration must start with uplift of the poor and masses. Maharaja or Raja of Khetri helped him go to America and financially during early wanderings. Went West to spread the true message of Vedanta. Chicago Parliament of Religions: message, all religions are true and lead to the same God; reaction, thunderous applause; style, humble yet commanding, rational yet spiritual; symbolises his universal outlook. (Answer key quirks: first recognition as world teacher, then key says His visit to Mysore; event that strengthened faith in India's spiritual power despite poverty, then key says Western admiration.) Founded Ramakrishna Mission (1897), headquarters Belur Math; motto Atmano mokshartham jagat hitaya cha; work means selfless service to poor, sick, needy as service to God; its founding means fusion of spirituality and service. 1899 Bengali journal Udbodhan. Final years and Mahasamadhi at Belur Math. Admired for eloquence, compassion, spiritual insight. Espoused all of the above: faith in yourself, never a life of secrecy, integrity.


People and quotes.

Swami Brahmananda: Ramakrishna's spiritual son. Swami Trigunatitananda: went to America to spread Vedanta. Sister Nivedita: schoolteacher from Ireland; He preached unto mankind their divinity.... Josephine MacLeod: friend and mother. Captain and Mrs Sevier: Advaita Ashrama, Mayavati. Chapter Some Men Who Were with Swamiji: gratitude and love for co-workers. Subhas Chandra Bose: maker of modern India. Rabindranath Tagore: Study Vivekananda to understand India. Sri Aurobindo: spiritual father of modern nationalism. Romain Rolland: greatest figure of the spiritual East; words are great music... Beethoven. John Henry Wright: a representative of all humanity. Max Müller: created a stir in America greater than any other Hindu. Mahatma Gandhi: love for country a thousand-fold, then deepened patriotism via spiritual ideals.

Quotes, then meaning: Arise, awake, and stop not till the goal is reached (awaken masses), Take up one idea..., then devote fully to a single purpose, The world is the great gymnasium..., then challenges build inner strength, All power is within you, then India's inherent strength must be awakened, Talk to yourself once in a day..., then self-reflection, Do not wait for anybody or anything, then personal effort and initiative, Be not afraid of anything..., then fearlessness, self-confidence, initiative.
