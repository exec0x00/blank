T1

EM radiation & interaction with matter

★★★
Definition

Molecular spectroscopy: the branch of science dealing with the interaction of electromagnetic radiation with matter (molecules).

★★★S001·3
Key point

Spectrophotometer: source → monochromator (entrance slit, dispersive element, exit slit) → sample → detector → recorder.

★★★S001·3
Key point

EM radiation has dual character (photons moving as waves); E and B fields oscillate perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation; all travel at the speed of light.

★★★S001·4
Key point

Atomic spectra: electronic transitions between atomic orbitals → line spectra. Molecular spectra: electronic (HOMO→LUMO) plus vibrational and rotational transitions → band spectra.

★★★S001·6-7
Key point

Absorption: molecule goes to a higher level taking ΔE = hν; emission: falls to a lower level releasing hν.

★★★S001
T2

Electromagnetic spectrum & regions

★★★
Key point

Radio (10 m–1 cm): NMR, ESR – nuclear / electron spin reversal. Microwave (1 cm–100 µm): rotational. IR (100 µm–1 µm): vibrational. UV-Vis (1 µm–10 nm): electronic, valence electrons. X-ray (10 nm–100 pm): inner electrons. γ (100–1 pm): nuclear rearrangement.

★★★S002·4-5
Why

Rayleigh scattering: scattered light has the same frequency as the incident light (elastic). Raman: shifted frequency — Stokes lines (lower frequency, molecule gains energy), anti-Stokes lines (higher frequency, molecule loses energy).

T3

Born–Oppenheimer approximation

★★★
Law
Born–Oppenheimer approximation: electronic, vibrational and rotational energies of a molecule are independent; E = Erot + Evib + Eelec (Etrans negligible).
★★★S003·4
Key point

Erot ≪ Evib ≪ Eelec because electronic levels are farther apart than vibrational, which are farther than rotational.

★★★S003·4
Exam trap

Mnemonic: Rot < Vib < Elec ↔ Microwave < IR < UV-Vis.

★★★study aidAdded for clarity
Asked in past ESA papers
  • (×3)State Born–Oppenheimer; energy-level diagram.

T4

Rotational (microwave) spectroscopy

★★★
Definition

Gross selection rule (rotational): molecule must have a permanent dipole moment. Specific rule: ΔJ = ±1.

★★★S004·3,10
Why

HCl and CO are microwave-active (permanent dipole); H₂, Cl₂ and CO₂ are not (no permanent dipole).

★★★S004·5
Key point

Rigid-rotor model: heteronuclear diatomic = two masses joined by a rigid massless rod; J = 0,1,2…; levels 0, 2B, 6B, 12B…

★★★S004·6-9
Key point

First line is at 2B (not B) and consecutive lines are 2B apart; B → I → r₀ via I = μr₀².

★★★S004·12
Key point

Intensity rises then falls with J because higher levels are populated at room temperature (thermal population).

★★★S004·12
Exam trap

Traps: J starts at 0; first line = 2B; use μ in kg (not amu); use c in cm s⁻¹ when B is in cm⁻¹.

★★★exam
Asked in past ESA papers
  • 2023–25 (×4)Derive I = μr₀² for a diatomic rigid rotor; calculate bond length from first line (HCl 21.18 cm⁻¹; HI 13.022 cm⁻¹); draw levels to J=5/6 and the spectrum.
T5

Vibrational (IR) – SHO

★★★
Definition

Gross selection rule (IR): the vibration must change the dipole moment; Δv = ±1.

★★★S005·3,9
Why

CO₂ has no permanent dipole (microwave-inactive) but is IR-active: asymmetric stretch and bending change the dipole; symmetric stretch does not.

★★★S005·4-5
Why

HI is IR-active but H₂ and I₂ are not (homonuclear: no dipole change).

★★★S005·4
Why

SHO shows only one line: levels are equally spaced and Δv = ±1, so every allowed transition has the same energy ν̄osc.

★★★S005·9
Key point

Zero-point energy ε₀ = ½ν̄osc ≠ 0: the molecule never rests even at v = 0. Bond treated as a spring obeying Hooke's law → parabolic PE.

★★★S005·6-9
Key point

Every bond has a characteristic frequency, so an IR spectrum is a molecular 'fingerprint'.

★★★S005·6
Asked in past ESA papers
  • 2023–25Calculate ν̄ and ZPE from k (HF, NO); k from ν̄ (CO); why only one line in SHO?
T6

Vibrational – anharmonic

★★★
Why

Why SHO fails: real bonds dissociate when stretched; the true potential is anharmonic (Morse), so levels converge and overtones appear.

★★★S006·3
Key point

Anharmonic: Δv = ±1, ±2, ±3…; most transitions start from v = 0 → fundamental (0→1), first overtone (0→2), second overtone (0→3).

★★★S006·6
Key point

Hot bands: from v = 1 at higher temperature; 1→2 appears to the left of the fundamental.

★★★S006·9
Key point

xₑ is small and positive; line spacing decreases with increasing v.

★★★S006·4
Asked in past ESA papers
  • 2023–25Find xₑ and ω̄ₑ from fundamental and overtone; selection rule; failure of SHO.
T7

Electronic spectroscopy & Franck–Condon

★★★
Key point

Electronic transitions: HOMO→LUMO (σ→σ*, π→π*, n→π*) in the UV-Vis; all molecules show them; visible transitions give colour.

★★★S007·3
Key point

Vibrational changes give 'coarse structure', rotational changes give 'fine structure'. Homonuclear molecules (H₂, N₂) show vibration/rotation only through electronic spectra.

★★★S007·5
Key point

No vibrational selection rule during electronic transitions; most start at v″ = 0; lines labelled (v′,v″) form a progression converging at high energy (anharmonic excited state).

★★★S007·6-8
Law
Franck–Condon principle: “An electronic transition takes place so rapidly that a vibrating molecule does not change its internuclear distance appreciably during the transition.” Hence transitions are vertical.
★★★S007·13
Key point

Case (a) rₑ′ = rₑ″: (0,0) strongest. Case (b) rₑ′ > rₑ″: strongest line (v′,0) with v′>0. Case (c) rₑ′ ≫ rₑ″: transition goes beyond the dissociation limit → continuum.

★★★S007·15-16
Why

A continuum means dissociation: the atoms fly apart with unquantised kinetic energy. The excited state usually has a longer bond length because of antibonding character.

★★★S007·10,16
Asked in past ESA papers
  • 2023–25 (×5)State the Franck–Condon principle; draw the three cases with spectra; meaning of a continuum.

T8

Basic terms & phase rule

★★★
Definition

Phase (P): homogeneous, physically distinct, mechanically separable part of a system bounded by a surface. Gases: P = 1; miscible liquids: P = 1; each solid / polymorph is a phase.

★★★S008·4
Definition

Component (C): smallest number of independently variable constituents by which the composition of every phase can be expressed (zero/negative values allowed).

★★★S008·6
Definition

Degrees of freedom (F): minimum number of intensive variables (T, P, concentration) to be fixed to define the system completely.

★★★S008·10
Law
Gibbs phase rule: provided equilibrium is not influenced by external forces (gravity, electrical, magnetic), F = C − P + 2.
★★★S008·12
Key point

Counting: ice/water/vapour P=3, C=1; CaCO₃ ⇌ CaO + CO₂: P=3, C=2; NH₄Cl ⇌ NH₃ + HCl: P=2, C=1 (C=2 if extra HCl/NH₃ added); Na₂SO₄·10H₂O system C=2.

★★★S008·5-9
Key point

Systems minimise Gibbs energy at constant T, P; at equilibrium G is equal in coexisting phases.

★★★S008·3
Asked in past ESA papers
  • (×2)Components and phases for CaCO₃/MgCO₃ decomposition.
T9

Derivation of phase rule

★★★
Key point

At equilibrium all phases have the same T and P and the chemical potential of each component is equal in every phase; mass flows from higher to lower μ.

★★★S009·3-4
Key point

Derivation: variables = P(C−1) composition terms + 2 (T,P); constraints = C(P−1); F = C−P+2.

★★★S009·7
Asked in past ESA papers
  • 2023–25 (×3)Derive the phase rule for C components and P phases.
T10

One-component system: water

★★★
Key point

Water: F = 3−P → one phase F=2 (bivariant area), two phases F=1 (univariant line), three phases F=0 (invariant point).

★★★S009·8-10
Key point

Triple point O: 0.0098 °C, 4.58 mmHg (F=0). Critical point A: 374 °C, 218 atm; above it water cannot be liquid.

★★★S009·15
Why

Fusion curve OC has negative slope: ice melts with decrease in volume (ΔV<0 → dP/dT<0), and it is steep because |ΔV| is small. Sublimation (OB) and vaporisation (OA) slopes are positive and less steep because ΔV is large.

★★★S009·12-14; T009bTranscript-assisted
Key point

Metastable OA′: supercooled water; its vapour pressure is higher than that of ice at the same T; disturbance returns the system to stable OB.

★★★S009·16
Asked in past ESA papers
  • 2023–25 (×4)Draw and label the water phase diagram; F in each region; metastable curve; negative slope of melting curve.
T11

Two-component: reduced phase rule & Pb–Ag

★★★
Why

General phase rule not used for 2 components: C=2 gives F=4−P, F=3 for P=1, needing 3-D; so pressure is fixed (≈1 atm) → F = C−P+1.

★★★S010·2-3
Definition

Condensed system: gas phase absent, hardly affected by small pressure changes.

★★★S010·3
Key point

Pb–Ag: components miscible in all proportions in melt, immiscible as solids; plot is temperature vs composition at constant pressure.

★★★S010·4
Key point

Freezing-point curves: C=2, P=2 → F=1 (univariant). Eutectic point O: P=3, F=0 (invariant), lowest freezing point, definite composition and sharp melting point.

★★★S010·7-8
Key point

Numerical facts: Pb mp 327 °C, Ag mp 961 °C, eutectic 303 °C at 97.4 % Pb / 2.6 % Ag.

★★★S010; PDF
Key point

Desilverisation of argentiferous lead (Pattinson-type process) exploits this: Pb crystallises first, leaving Ag-rich melt.

★★★T010Transcript only
Asked in past ESA papers
  • 2023–25Draw the Pb–Ag diagram with eutectic values; why F = C−P+1.
T12

Thermal analysis / cooling curves

★★★
Definition

Thermal analysis: study of cooling curves (temperature vs time) of various compositions during solidification; freezing and eutectic points are read from them.

★★★S012·2
Key point

Pure substance: smooth fall, then a flat halt at the freezing point. Mixture: a change of slope when crystallisation starts, then a halt at the eutectic temperature. Plotting break/halt temperatures against composition builds the phase diagram.

Computational chemistry

★★★
Definition

Computational chemistry: techniques to solve chemical problems by simulating chemical systems, giving atomic-level information, using theoretical chemistry in efficient programs.

★★★S011·3
Key point

First theoretical work: Heitler and London, 1927. (Nobel 1998 Kohn & Pople; 2013 Karplus, Levitt & Warshel.)

★★★S011·3; briefAdded for clarity
Key point

Scope: molecular properties (HOMO/LUMO, UV/IR/NMR spectra), reaction mechanism and kinetics, material design, drug discovery.

★★★S011·6
Key point

Classical: molecular mechanics (balls and springs, very fast) and molecular dynamics (motion of atoms). Quantum: semi-empirical, ab initio (wavefunction) and DFT (electron density; faster than ab initio, slower than semi-empirical).

★★★S011·7-12
Key point

Advantages: easier, cheaper, safer than experiments; complements, not replaces, experiment.

★★★S011·13
Key point

Ethylene: sp² C; σ + π bond; HOMO = π, LUMO = π*. Steps: geometry → basis set → method → solve → visualise.

★★★S011·14-17
Key point

AI-enhanced: fast screening, property prediction, reaction-pathway mapping, structure generation; speed-up from hours/days to milliseconds after training.

★★★S011·19-20
