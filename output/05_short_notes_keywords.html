T1

EM radiation & interaction with matter

★★★
Short note

Spectroscopy studies radiation–matter interaction. A spectrophotometer has source, monochromator, sample, detector, recorder. EM waves have E ⟂ B ⟂ direction. E = hν, ν = c/λ, ν̄ = 1/λ. Atomic spectra are lines; molecular spectra are bands.

  • absorption spectrum — radiation absorbed as molecule moves to higher level
  • band spectrum — molecular spectrum from electronic+vib+rot transitions
  • wavenumber — 1/λ in cm⁻¹
T2

Electromagnetic spectrum & regions

★★★
Short note

Radio→NMR/ESR; microwave→rotation; IR→vibration; UV-Vis→valence electrons; X-ray→inner electrons; γ→nuclei. Raman: Rayleigh (no shift), Stokes (lower ν), anti-Stokes (higher ν).

  • Rayleigh scattering — elastic scattering
  • Stokes line — Raman line at lower frequency
  • anti-Stokes line — Raman line at higher frequency
T3

Born–Oppenheimer approximation

★★★
Short note

E = Erot + Evib + Eelec, Erot ≪ Evib ≪ Eelec, components independent; translation negligible.

Beer–Lambert law

★★★
Short note

A = log(I₀/I) = εcl (older syllabus; not on current slides).

T4

Rotational (microwave) spectroscopy

★★★
Short note

Rigid rotor: two masses at fixed r₀. I = μr₀². εJ = BJ(J+1). Needs permanent dipole; ΔJ=±1. Lines at 2B(J+1), spacing 2B. From B get I and r₀.

  • centre of mass — point where m₁r₁ = m₂r₂
  • gross selection rule — dipole requirement
  • moment of inertia — I = μr₀²
  • reduced mass — μ = m₁m₂/(m₁+m₂)
  • rigid rotor — fixed-r₀ model
  • rotational constant — B = h/8π²Ic
T5

Vibrational (IR) – SHO

★★★
Short note

SHO: spring/Hooke's law, parabolic PE. εv = (v+½)ν̄, ZPE = ½ν̄, equal spacing, Δv=±1 → one line at ν̄. Needs changing dipole; ν̄ = (1/2πc)√(k/μ).

  • Hooke's law — F = −kx
  • force constant — k, N m⁻¹
  • zero-point energy — ½ν̄osc
T6

Vibrational – anharmonic

★★★
Short note

Morse potential: εv = (v+½)ω̄ₑ − (v+½)²ω̄ₑxₑ. Levels converge; Δv=±1,±2…; fundamental, overtones; hot bands left of fundamental.

  • Morse function — anharmonic PE
  • anharmonicity constant xₑ — small positive constant in Morse levels
  • fundamental — v=0→1
  • hot band — transition from v=1
  • overtone — v=0→2, 3…
T7

Electronic spectroscopy & Franck–Condon

★★★
Short note

HOMO→LUMO transitions; vibrational coarse structure gives progressions (v′,v″); no vibrational selection rule; Franck–Condon: vertical transitions → intensity pattern by rₑ′ vs rₑ″; continuum = dissociation.

  • HOMO / LUMO — frontier orbitals
  • coarse structure — vibrational structure on electronic bands
  • continuum — non-quantised dissociation absorption
  • fine structure — rotational structure
  • progression — series (v′,0) lines
  • vertical transition — Franck–Condon

T8

Basic terms & phase rule

★★★
Short note

Phase = homogeneous, separable part; component = min. independent constituents; F = min. intensive variables. F = C−P+2.

  • Gibbs phase rule — F = C−P+2
  • bivariant — F = 2
  • component — min. independent constituents
  • degrees of freedom — F
  • invariant — F = 0
  • phase — homogeneous separable part
  • univariant — F = 1
T9

Derivation of phase rule

★★★
Short note

Variables P(C−1)+2, constraints C(P−1), equal μ in all phases → F = C−P+2.

  • chemical potential — (∂G/∂nᵢ); equal in all phases at equilibrium
T10

One-component system: water

★★★
Short note

F = 3−P. Areas ice/water/vapour F=2; lines OA, OB, OC F=1; triple point O F=0 (0.0098 °C, 4.58 mmHg); critical 374 °C, 218 atm; OC slope negative; OA′ metastable.

  • Clausius–Clapeyron — dP/dT = ΔH/TΔV
  • critical point — 374 °C, 218 atm
  • metastable — supercooled water, OA′
  • triple point — 0.0098 °C, 4.58 mmHg
T11

Two-component: reduced phase rule & Pb–Ag

★★★
Short note

Condensed system at fixed P: F = C−P+1. Pb–Ag eutectic: 303 °C, 2.6 % Ag, F=0; curves F=1.

  • condensed system — no gas phase
  • eutectic — lowest-melting mixture, F=0
  • reduced phase rule — F = C−P+1
T12

Thermal analysis / cooling curves

★★★
Short note

Cooling curves: halt for pure/eutectic, slope break for mixtures; plot to build phase diagram.

  • thermal analysis — cooling-curve study

T13

Computational chemistry

★★★
Short note

Computational chemistry simulates molecules: MM/MD (classical), semi-empirical, ab initio, DFT (quantum). Cheap, safe; AI adds speed.

  • DFT — uses electron density
  • ab initio — wavefunction-based
  • molecular dynamics — atomic motion simulation
  • molecular mechanics — balls and springs
  • semi-empirical — approximate Schrödinger

Last 10 minutes before the exam

  1. E = hν, ν = c/λ, ν̄ = 1/λ
  2. Erot ≪ Evib ≪ Eelec (microwave < IR < UV-Vis)
  3. εJ = BJ(J+1); lines at 2B(J+1); spacing 2B; first line at 2B
  4. I = μr₀² → r₀ = √(I/μ), μ in kg
  5. Microwave needs a permanent dipole, ΔJ = ±1
  6. ν̄ = (1/2πc)√(k/μ); εv = (v+½)ν̄; ZPE = ½ν̄
  7. IR needs a changing dipole; SHO gives one line (Δv = ±1)
  8. Fundamental ω̄ₑ(1−2xₑ); 1st overtone 2ω̄ₑ(1−3xₑ)
  9. Franck–Condon: vertical transitions; 3 cases; continuum = dissociation
  10. F = C − P + 2 from P(C−1)+2 − C(P−1)
  11. Water: F = 3 − P; triple point 0.0098 °C, 4.58 mmHg; critical 374 °C, 218 atm
  12. OC slope negative because ice → water shrinks (ΔV < 0)
  13. Two components at fixed P: F = C − P + 1
  14. Pb–Ag eutectic 303 °C, 2.6 % Ag, F = 0; Pb 327 °C, Ag 961 °C
