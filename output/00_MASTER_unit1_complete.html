M1 · Molecular Spectroscopy

T1 · EM radiation & interaction with matter

Short note

Spectroscopy studies radiation–matter interaction. A spectrophotometer has source, monochromator, sample, detector, recorder. EM waves have E ⟂ B ⟂ direction. E = hν, ν = c/λ, ν̄ = 1/λ. Atomic spectra are lines; molecular spectra are bands.

Formulas

★★★
F1 · Photon energy
E = hν
Gives Energy of radiation · Applies any EM radiation · Units J · [S001·5]
★★★
F2 · Frequency–wavelength
ν = c/λ
Gives Frequency · Applies vacuum/air · Units Hz · [S001·5]
★★★
F3 · Wavenumber
ν̄ = 1/λ ; E = hcν̄
Gives Wavenumber; energy · Applies spectroscopic unit · Units cm⁻¹ · [S001·5]
★★★
F4 · Transition energy
ΔE = E₂ − E₁ = hν
Gives Absorbed/emitted quantum · Applies two quantised levels · Units J (÷hc → cm⁻¹) · [S001]

Important points

★★Definition: Molecular spectroscopy: the branch of science dealing with the interaction of electromagnetic radiation with matter (molecules). [S001·3]
★★Spectrophotometer: source → monochromator (entrance slit, dispersive element, exit slit) → sample → detector → recorder. [S001·3]
★★EM radiation has dual character (photons moving as waves); E and B fields oscillate perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation; all travel at the speed of light. [S001·4]
★★Atomic spectra: electronic transitions between atomic orbitals → line spectra. Molecular spectra: electronic (HOMO→LUMO) plus vibrational and rotational transitions → band spectra. [S001·6-7]
★Absorption: molecule goes to a higher level taking ΔE = hν; emission: falls to a lower level releasing hν. [S001]

T2 · Electromagnetic spectrum & regions

Short note

Radio→NMR/ESR; microwave→rotation; IR→vibration; UV-Vis→valence electrons; X-ray→inner electrons; γ→nuclei. Raman: Rayleigh (no shift), Stokes (lower ν), anti-Stokes (higher ν).

Important points

★★★Radio (10 m–1 cm): NMR, ESR – nuclear / electron spin reversal. Microwave (1 cm–100 µm): rotational. IR (100 µm–1 µm): vibrational. UV-Vis (1 µm–10 nm): electronic, valence electrons. X-ray (10 nm–100 pm): inner electrons. γ (100–1 pm): nuclear rearrangement. [S002·4-5]
★★Why: Rayleigh scattering: scattered light has the same frequency as the incident light (elastic). Raman: shifted frequency — Stokes lines (lower frequency, molecule gains energy), anti-Stokes lines (higher frequency, molecule loses energy). [T002] Transcript onlyAdded for clarity

T3 · Born–Oppenheimer approximation

Short note

E = E_rot + E_vib + E_elec, E_rot ≪ E_vib ≪ E_elec, components independent; translation negligible.

Formulas

★★
F5 · Total molecular energy
E = E_trans + E_rot + E_vib + E_elec ≈ E_rot + E_vib + E_elec
Gives Born–Oppenheimer · Applies E_trans negligible · Units J · [S003·4]
★★
F6 · Ordering
E_rot ≪ E_vib ≪ E_elec
Gives Microwave < IR < UV-Vis · Applies — · Units — · [S003·4]
★★
F7 · Change in energy
ΔE = ΔE_elec + ΔE_vib + ΔE_rot
Gives Electronic transition fine structure · Applies — · Units J or cm⁻¹ · [S007·4]

Important points

★★Statement: Born–Oppenheimer approximation: electronic, vibrational and rotational energies of a molecule are independent; E = E_rot + E_vib + E_elec (E_trans negligible). [S003·4]
★★E_rot ≪ E_vib ≪ E_elec because electronic levels are farther apart than vibrational, which are farther than rotational. [S003·4]
★Exam trap: Mnemonic: Rot < Vib < Elec ↔ Microwave < IR < UV-Vis. [study aid] Added for clarity

T4 · Rotational (microwave) spectroscopy

Short note

Rigid rotor: two masses at fixed r₀. I = μr₀². ε_J = BJ(J+1). Needs permanent dipole; ΔJ=±1. Lines at 2B(J+1), spacing 2B. From B get I and r₀.

Formulas

★★★
F9 · Centre of mass
m₁r₁ = m₂r₂ ; r₀ = r₁ + r₂
Gives Positions of atoms about C · Applies rigid diatomic · Units m · [S004; PDF]
★★★
F10 · Distances
r₁ = m₂r₀/(m₁+m₂) ; r₂ = m₁r₀/(m₁+m₂)
Gives — · Applies — · Units m · [PDF]
★★★
F11 · Moment of inertia
I = m₁r₁² + m₂r₂² = μr₀² with μ = m₁m₂/(m₁+m₂)
Gives Moment of inertia · Applies rigid rotor · Units kg m² · [S004·7; PDF]
★★
F12 · Rotational energy
E_r = ½Iω² = L²/2I
Gives Classical rotor · Applies — · Units J · [PDF]
★★★
F13 · Rotational levels (J)
E_J = h²J(J+1)/(8π²I)
Gives Quantised levels · Applies J = 0,1,2… · Units J · [S004·8]
★★★
F14 · Rotational levels (cm⁻¹)
ε_J = BJ(J+1), B = h/(8π²Ic)
Gives Level energy; rotational constant · Applies c in cm s⁻¹ · Units cm⁻¹ · [S004·8]
★★★
F15 · Selection rules
ΔJ = ±1 ; permanent dipole needed
Gives Allowed transitions · Applies gross rule · Units — · [S004·10]
★★★
F16 · Line positions
Δε(J→J+1) = 2B(J+1) ; spacing = 2B ; levels 0,2B,6B,12B,20B,30B,42B
Gives Rotational spectrum · Applies — · Units cm⁻¹ · [S004·9-10]

Important points

★★★Definition: Gross selection rule (rotational): molecule must have a permanent dipole moment. Specific rule: ΔJ = ±1. [S004·3,10]
★★★Why: HCl and CO are microwave-active (permanent dipole); H₂, Cl₂ and CO₂ are not (no permanent dipole). [S004·5]
★★★Rigid-rotor model: heteronuclear diatomic = two masses joined by a rigid massless rod; J = 0,1,2…; levels 0, 2B, 6B, 12B… [S004·6-9]
★★★First line is at 2B (not B) and consecutive lines are 2B apart; B → I → r₀ via I = μr₀². [S004·12]
★★Intensity rises then falls with J because higher levels are populated at room temperature (thermal population). [S004·12]
★★★Exam trap: Traps: J starts at 0; first line = 2B; use μ in kg (not amu); use c in cm s⁻¹ when B is in cm⁻¹. [exam]

T5 · Vibrational (IR) – SHO

Short note

SHO: spring/Hooke's law, parabolic PE. ε_v = (v+½)ν̄, ZPE = ½ν̄, equal spacing, Δv=±1 → one line at ν̄. Needs changing dipole; ν̄ = (1/2πc)√(k/μ).

Formulas

★★
F17 · Hooke's law
F = −kx ; PE = ½k(r − r_eq)²
Gives Parabolic potential · Applies SHO · Units N ; J · [S005·6-7]
★★★
F18 · Oscillation frequency
ν_osc = (1/2π)√(k/μ)
Gives Frequency · Applies SHO · Units Hz · [S005·8]
★★★
F19 · Wavenumber of vibration
ν̄_osc = (1/2πc)√(k/μ)
Gives Line position · Applies c in cm s⁻¹ · Units cm⁻¹ · [S005·8]
★★★
F20 · SHO levels
E_v = (v+½)hν_osc ; ε_v = (v+½)ν̄_osc
Gives Levels · Applies v = 0,1,2… · Units J ; cm⁻¹ · [S005·8-9]
★★★
F21 · Zero-point energy
ε₀ = ½ν̄_osc
Gives Lowest level (≠ 0) · Applies — · Units cm⁻¹ · [S005·9]
★★★
F22 · Selection rule & spacing
Δv = ±1 ; spacing = ν̄_osc ; one line at ν̄_osc
Gives — · Applies dipole must change · Units cm⁻¹ · [S005·9]
★★★
F23 · Force constant
k = 4π²c²ν̄²μ
Gives Bond stiffness · Applies μ in kg · Units N m⁻¹ · [derived]

Important points

★★★Definition: Gross selection rule (IR): the vibration must change the dipole moment; Δv = ±1. [S005·3,9]
★★★Why: CO₂ has no permanent dipole (microwave-inactive) but is IR-active: asymmetric stretch and bending change the dipole; symmetric stretch does not. [S005·4-5]
★★Why: HI is IR-active but H₂ and I₂ are not (homonuclear: no dipole change). [S005·4]
★★★Why: SHO shows only one line: levels are equally spaced and Δv = ±1, so every allowed transition has the same energy ν̄_osc. [S005·9]
★★★Zero-point energy ε₀ = ½ν̄_osc ≠ 0: the molecule never rests even at v = 0. Bond treated as a spring obeying Hooke's law → parabolic PE. [S005·6-9]
★Every bond has a characteristic frequency, so an IR spectrum is a molecular 'fingerprint'. [S005·6]

T6 · Vibrational – anharmonic

Short note

Morse potential: ε_v = (v+½)ω̄ₑ − (v+½)²ω̄ₑxₑ. Levels converge; Δv=±1,±2…; fundamental, overtones; hot bands left of fundamental.

Formulas

★★
F24 · Morse function
PE = D_eq[1 − exp{a(r_eq − r)}]²
Gives Anharmonic potential · Applies — · Units J · [S006·3]
★★
F25 · Anharmonic levels
ε_v = (v+½)ω̄ₑ − (v+½)²ω̄ₑxₑ
Gives Converging levels · Applies xₑ small, positive · Units cm⁻¹ · [S006·4]
★★
F26 · Transitions
Fundamental 0→1: ω̄ₑ(1−2xₑ) ; 1st overtone 0→2: 2ω̄ₑ(1−3xₑ) ; 2nd overtone 0→3: 3ω̄ₑ(1−4xₑ)
Gives — · Applies Δv = ±1,±2,±3… · Units cm⁻¹ · [S006·6-7]
★★
F27 · Hot band & ZPE Added for clarity
Hot band 1→2: ω̄ₑ(1−4xₑ) (left of fundamental) ; ε₀ = ½ω̄ₑ(1−½xₑ)
Gives — · Applies higher T · Units cm⁻¹ · [S006·9; ZPE added]

Important points

★★Why: Why SHO fails: real bonds dissociate when stretched; the true potential is anharmonic (Morse), so levels converge and overtones appear. [S006·3]
★★Anharmonic: Δv = ±1, ±2, ±3…; most transitions start from v = 0 → fundamental (0→1), first overtone (0→2), second overtone (0→3). [S006·6]
★★Hot bands: from v = 1 at higher temperature; 1→2 appears to the left of the fundamental. [S006·9]
★★xₑ is small and positive; line spacing decreases with increasing v. [S006·4]

T7 · Electronic spectroscopy & Franck–Condon

Short note

HOMO→LUMO transitions; vibrational coarse structure gives progressions (v′,v″); no vibrational selection rule; Franck–Condon: vertical transitions → intensity pattern by rₑ′ vs rₑ″; continuum = dissociation.

Formulas

★★
F28 · Electronic spectrum
ε = ε_elec + ε_vib ; lines labelled (v′,v″)
Gives Progression · Applies no vibrational selection rule · Units cm⁻¹ · [S007·6-8]

Important points

★★★Electronic transitions: HOMO→LUMO (σ→σ*, π→π*, n→π*) in the UV-Vis; all molecules show them; visible transitions give colour. [S007·3]
★★Vibrational changes give 'coarse structure', rotational changes give 'fine structure'. Homonuclear molecules (H₂, N₂) show vibration/rotation only through electronic spectra. [S007·5]
★★★No vibrational selection rule during electronic transitions; most start at v″ = 0; lines labelled (v′,v″) form a progression converging at high energy (anharmonic excited state). [S007·6-8]
★★★Statement: Franck–Condon principle: “An electronic transition takes place so rapidly that a vibrating molecule does not change its internuclear distance appreciably during the transition.” Hence transitions are vertical. [S007·13]
★★★Case (a) rₑ′ = rₑ″: (0,0) strongest. Case (b) rₑ′ > rₑ″: strongest line (v′,0) with v′>0. Case (c) rₑ′ ≫ rₑ″: transition goes beyond the dissociation limit → continuum. [S007·15-16]
★★★Why: A continuum means dissociation: the atoms fly apart with unquantised kinetic energy. The excited state usually has a longer bond length because of antibonding character. [S007·10,16]

F7 · Franck–Condon outcome

  1. Compare rₑ′ with rₑ″
  2. Equal → (0,0) strongest
  3. Longer → (v′,0), v′>0 strongest
  4. Much longer → continuum (dissociation)

M2 · Phase Equilibria

T8 · Basic terms & phase rule

Short note

Phase = homogeneous, separable part; component = min. independent constituents; F = min. intensive variables. F = C−P+2.

Formulas

★★★
F29 · Phase rule
F = C − P + 2
Gives Degrees of freedom · Applies no external fields · Units — · [S008·12]

Important points

★★★Definition: Phase (P): homogeneous, physically distinct, mechanically separable part of a system bounded by a surface. Gases: P = 1; miscible liquids: P = 1; each solid / polymorph is a phase. [S008·4]
★★★Definition: Component (C): smallest number of independently variable constituents by which the composition of every phase can be expressed (zero/negative values allowed). [S008·6]
★★★Definition: Degrees of freedom (F): minimum number of intensive variables (T, P, concentration) to be fixed to define the system completely. [S008·10]
★★★Statement: Gibbs phase rule: provided equilibrium is not influenced by external forces (gravity, electrical, magnetic), F = C − P + 2. [S008·12]
★★Counting: ice/water/vapour P=3, C=1; CaCO₃ ⇌ CaO + CO₂: P=3, C=2; NH₄Cl ⇌ NH₃ + HCl: P=2, C=1 (C=2 if extra HCl/NH₃ added); Na₂SO₄·10H₂O system C=2. [S008·5-9]
★Systems minimise Gibbs energy at constant T, P; at equilibrium G is equal in coexisting phases. [S008·3]

Flowcharts & tables

F9 · Phase-rule application

  1. Count P
  2. Count C (minimum constituents)
  3. Gas absent & constant pressure? → F=C−P+1, else F=C−P+2
  4. Classify F=2 bivariant, 1 univariant, 0 invariant

T9 · Derivation of phase rule

Short note

Variables P(C−1)+2, constraints C(P−1), equal μ in all phases → F = C−P+2.

Formulas

★★★
F30 · Variables & constraints
Variables = P(C−1)+2 ; constraints = C(P−1) ; F = P(C−1)+2 − C(P−1) = C−P+2
Gives Derivation · Applies — · Units — · [S009·7]
★★★
F31 · Equilibrium condition
μᵢ^α = μᵢ^β = μᵢ^γ = …
Gives Equal chemical potential · Applies each component · Units J mol⁻¹ · [S009·3,7]
★★
F32 · Chemical potential Transcript only

Gives Partial molar Gibbs energy · Applies — · Units J mol⁻¹ · [T008 (S009·5 unreadable)]

Important points

★★★At equilibrium all phases have the same T and P and the chemical potential of each component is equal in every phase; mass flows from higher to lower μ. [S009·3-4]
★★★Derivation: variables = P(C−1) composition terms + 2 (T,P); constraints = C(P−1); F = C−P+2. [S009·7]

T10 · One-component system: water

Short note

F = 3−P. Areas ice/water/vapour F=2; lines OA, OB, OC F=1; triple point O F=0 (0.0098 °C, 4.58 mmHg); critical 374 °C, 218 atm; OC slope negative; OA′ metastable.

Formulas

★★★
F33 · One-component
F = 3 − P
Gives Area F=2, line F=1, point F=0 · Applies — · Units — · [S009·8]
★★★
F34 · Clausius–Clapeyron
dP/dT = ΔH/(TΔV)
Gives Slope of curve · Applies fusion, vaporisation, sublimation · Units Pa K⁻¹ · [S009·12-14]

Important points

★★★Water: F = 3−P → one phase F=2 (bivariant area), two phases F=1 (univariant line), three phases F=0 (invariant point). [S009·8-10]
★★★Triple point O: 0.0098 °C, 4.58 mmHg (F=0). Critical point A: 374 °C, 218 atm; above it water cannot be liquid. [S009·15]
★★★Why: Fusion curve OC has negative slope: ice melts with decrease in volume (ΔV<0 → dP/dT<0), and it is steep because |ΔV| is small. Sublimation (OB) and vaporisation (OA) slopes are positive and less steep because ΔV is large. [S009·12-14; T009b] Transcript-assisted
★★★Metastable OA′: supercooled water; its vapour pressure is higher than that of ice at the same T; disturbance returns the system to stable OB. [S009·16]

T11 · Two-component: reduced phase rule & Pb–Ag

Short note

Condensed system at fixed P: F = C−P+1. Pb–Ag eutectic: 303 °C, 2.6 % Ag, F=0; curves F=1.

Formulas

★★★
F35 · Reduced (condensed) rule
F = C − P + 1
Gives Constant pressure · Applies condensed systems · Units — · [S010·2-3]

Important points

★★★Why: General phase rule not used for 2 components: C=2 gives F=4−P, F=3 for P=1, needing 3-D; so pressure is fixed (≈1 atm) → F = C−P+1. [S010·2-3]
★★Definition: Condensed system: gas phase absent, hardly affected by small pressure changes. [S010·3]
★★★Pb–Ag: components miscible in all proportions in melt, immiscible as solids; plot is temperature vs composition at constant pressure. [S010·4]
★★★Freezing-point curves: C=2, P=2 → F=1 (univariant). Eutectic point O: P=3, F=0 (invariant), lowest freezing point, definite composition and sharp melting point. [S010·7-8]
★★★Numerical facts: Pb mp 327 °C, Ag mp 961 °C, eutectic 303 °C at 97.4 % Pb / 2.6 % Ag. [S010; PDF]
★Desilverisation of argentiferous lead (Pattinson-type process) exploits this: Pb crystallises first, leaving Ag-rich melt. [T010] Transcript only

T12 · Thermal analysis / cooling curves

Short note

Cooling curves: halt for pure/eutectic, slope break for mixtures; plot to build phase diagram.

Important points

★★Definition: Thermal analysis: study of cooling curves (temperature vs time) of various compositions during solidification; freezing and eutectic points are read from them. [S012·2]
★★Pure substance: smooth fall, then a flat halt at the freezing point. Mixture: a change of slope when crystallisation starts, then a halt at the eutectic temperature. Plotting break/halt temperatures against composition builds the phase diagram. [S012·2-3]

M3 · Computational Chemistry

T13 · Computational chemistry

Short note

Computational chemistry simulates molecules: MM/MD (classical), semi-empirical, ab initio, DFT (quantum). Cheap, safe; AI adds speed.

Important points

★Definition: Computational chemistry: techniques to solve chemical problems by simulating chemical systems, giving atomic-level information, using theoretical chemistry in efficient programs. [S011·3]
★First theoretical work: Heitler and London, 1927. (Nobel 1998 Kohn & Pople; 2013 Karplus, Levitt & Warshel.) [S011·3; brief] Added for clarity
★Scope: molecular properties (HOMO/LUMO, UV/IR/NMR spectra), reaction mechanism and kinetics, material design, drug discovery. [S011·6]
★Classical: molecular mechanics (balls and springs, very fast) and molecular dynamics (motion of atoms). Quantum: semi-empirical, ab initio (wavefunction) and DFT (electron density; faster than ab initio, slower than semi-empirical). [S011·7-12]
★Advantages: easier, cheaper, safer than experiments; complements, not replaces, experiment. [S011·13]
★Ethylene: sp² C; σ + π bond; HOMO = π, LUMO = π*. Steps: geometry → basis set → method → solve → visualise. [S011·14-17]
★AI-enhanced: fast screening, property prediction, reaction-pathway mapping, structure generation; speed-up from hours/days to milliseconds after training. [S011·19-20]
