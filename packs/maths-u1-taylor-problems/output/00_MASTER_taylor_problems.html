1Taylor expansion about a point (a, b)

Write h=x−a, k=y−b, tabulate f and its partial derivatives, evaluate them at (a,b) and substitute into f+{hfx​+kfy​}+2!1​{h2fxx​+2hkfxy​+k2fyy​}+3!1​{…}. “In powers of (x−1) and (y−4π​)” means about (1,4π​). Class 008 works excosy, sin(xy) and xy2+ycos(x−y); the ESA favourite is sinxsiny about (4π​,4π​).

Key terms and definitions

Expansion “in powers of (x − a) and (y − b)”. Taylor's series of f(x,y) about the point (a,b). S008·3 · T008 00:00:37

Important points and why

  1. Write the general series first, then find the derivatives, then substitute. That is the order the lecture follows. ESA ★★ T008 00:00:21 · T008 00:02:07
  2. For excosy at (1,4π​) every derivative is ±2​e​; only the signs differ, so take 2​e​ out as a common factor. ESA ★★★ T008 00:04:54 · T008 00:05:19
  3. If the question asks for third degree, you need the third-order partials fxxx​,fxxy​,fxyy​,fyyy​ too. ESA ★★★ T008 00:06:10
  4. Derivatives that vanish at the point drop whole terms: for sin(xy) at (1,2π​) both first partials are 0. ESA ★★ S008·15 · S008·16
  5. Differentiate carefully with the product and chain rules; the slide's own fyy​ for xy2+ycos(x−y) has a sign slip (see the exam traps). ESA ★★ added for clarity S008·17 · S008·18
Exam trap. For xy2+ycos(x−y): fyy​=2x+2sin(x−y)−ycos(x−y), so fyy​(1,1)=1, not 3 as slide 008·18 prints. added for clarity S008·17 · S008·18

2Maclaurin expansion about the origin

The same method with (a,b)=(0,0), so h=x, k=y. Class 008 works log(2x+y+1), ex−y and exlog(1+y); the ESA papers ask log(1+x−y), eaxsinby and sin(x+2y), all up to third degree. When f depends on one combination such as 2x+y or x−y, the one-variable series gives the answer at once and is the best check.

Key terms and definitions

Up to three terms. Keep going until there are three non-zero terms: “make sure that you will get three non-zero terms”. S008·5 · T008 00:07:38
“Taylor's series about (0,0) is given by f(x,y)≈f(0,0)+fx​(0,0)x+fy​(0,0)y+2!1​[fxx​(0,0)x2+2fxy​(0,0)xy+fyy​(0,0)y2]+3!1​[fxxx​(0,0)x3+3fxxy​(0,0)x2y+3fxyy​(0,0)xy2+fyyy​(0,0)y3]+…” S008·10

Important points and why

  1. About (0,0) the constant term is often 0 (log1=0, sin0=0), so the series starts at first degree. ESA ★★ T008 00:07:57
  2. “Up to three terms” means three non-zero terms; the lecture still writes all second-order terms. ESA ★★ T008 00:07:38 · T008 00:10:55
  3. Collect and simplify at the end: 2x+y−2x2−2xy−2y2​. ESA ★ T008 00:11:11
  4. Check a two-variable answer with the one-variable series of the combination (E2.2, Figure G2.1). ESA ★★★ added for clarity S008·6 · S008·11
Why does log(2x+y+1) give (2x+y)−2(2x+y)2​+…?
It is log(1+t) with t=2x+y, and log(1+t)=t−2t2​+3t3​−…; the two-variable Maclaurin series must agree with it.

3Approximate values from a Taylor polynomial

To estimate f at an awkward point, expand about a nearby point where everything is known, then substitute the small h and k. Class 008 estimates tan−1(xy) at (0.9,−1.2) from (1,−1); the class-work sheet estimates tan−11.10.9​ from (1,1). More terms give a better value.

Key terms and definitions

Approximate value. The value of a truncated Taylor polynomial at a point near the expansion point. S008·7 · T008 00:11:47

givesA numerical estimate.appliesh, k small; (a,b) a point where f and its derivatives are easy.noteChoose (a,b) with “nice” values: (1,−1) for (0.9,−1.2).

Flowcharts

Important points and why

  1. Pick an expansion point near the target where f is easy: (1,−1) for (0.9,−1.2), (1,1) for (1.1,0.9). ESA ★★ T008 00:12:10 · CW U1 P26
  2. “The more terms you take, the better the approximation”: the first-order estimate of tan−1(−1.08) is off by 0.012, the second-order one by 0.001. ESA ★★ T008 00:14:18
  3. Substitute h=x−a and k=y−b with their signs: x=0.9 gives h=−0.1, y=−1.2 gives k=−0.2. ESA ★★ T008 00:15:13 · T008 00:15:28
Why expand about (1, −1) to evaluate at (0.9, −1.2)?
At (1,−1) every value is exact (tan−1(−1)=−4π​, simple derivatives), and h=−0.1, k=−0.2 are small, so the higher terms shrink quickly.

Eight past ESA papers were checked: UE22MA141A Dec 2023, Jul 2023 and May 2024, and UE23MA141A Jan 2024 sets 1–4 and June 2024. A two-variable Taylor or Maclaurin expansion is asked in six of them, always a 6–7 mark Q1 part: Jul 2023 Q1.b and Dec 2023 Q1.b (sinxsiny about (4π​,4π​), second order and third degree), May 2024 Q1.b (excosy about (1,4π​), the same as slide 008·3), Jan 2024 set 1 Q1.b (log(1+x−y)), set 2 Q1.b (eaxsinby) and June 2024 Q1.c (sin(x+2y)). Four ask for third-degree terms. Jan 2024 sets 3 and 4 have no Taylor question. Approximate values (topic 3) were never asked in these papers; they appear in class and in CW problem 26.

Known gaps, conflicts and corrections

  1. Slide 008·18 gives fyy​(1,1)=3 for xy2+ycos(x−y); it is 1. Slide 008·17's fyy​ has the wrong sign on ycos(x−y): the correct fyy​=2x+2sin(x−y)−ycos(x−y). Slide 008·19's final answer (with fyy​=1) is correct.
  2. Slide 008·12 lists fxy​(0,0)=0 for exlog(1+y); it is 1 (fxy​=1+yex​). Slide 008·13 uses the correct value.
  3. Slide 008·12 asks for “the first six terms” of exlog(1+y) but slide 008·13 stops after three (y+xy−2y2​). The six terms y+xy−2y2​+2x2y​−2xy2​+3y3​ are added for clarity (sympy).
  4. Slide 008·11 computes the third-order derivatives of ex−y but stops the series at second order; the third-degree term 6(x−y)3​ is added for clarity.
  5. Slide 008·9 approximates tan−1(0.9×(−1.2)) with first-order terms only: −4π​−0.05=−0.8354. With the second-order terms of slide 008·8 it is −0.8229; the exact value is −0.8238 (sympy). Both are given.
  6. Slides 008·6, 008·8 and 008·9 are partly garbled in the rendering; they were rebuilt from the transcript (T008 00:10:35, 00:14:35, 00:15:42) and checked by recomputing.
  7. Slide 008·3 does not state the degree; the lecture says “up to second degree” (T008 00:00:21).
  8. The lecture twice reads fxx​(1,4π​) and fxy​(1,4π​) as “e divided by two” (T008 00:03:47, 00:04:16); every value on slide 008·3 is ±2​e​.
  9. The lecture says “f(0.9,−1.2) is approximately equal to f(1,−1)” (T008 00:12:10). That only motivates the choice of the expansion point; the approximation is the Taylor polynomial, not f(1,−1)=−0.785.
  10. The lecture works slides 3–9 only (16.2 min). Slides 10–19 (ex−y, exlog(1+y), sin(xy), xy2+ycos(x−y)) have no recorded explanation.
  11. The class 007 theory is in its own pack; the formulas are repeated here without derivation.
  12. Transcript recognition errors were corrected silently: “McLaren” / “my cleric series” → Maclaurin series, “e bar root” / “e pi root 2” → e/2​, “1 comma 5 by 4” → (1,4π​), “y minus 4” → y−4π​.
