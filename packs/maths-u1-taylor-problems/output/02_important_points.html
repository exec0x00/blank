1Taylor expansion about a point (a, b)

Definitions

Expansion “in powers of (x − a) and (y − b)”. Taylor's series of f(x,y) about the point (a,b). S008·3 · T008 00:00:37
Up to n-th degree terms. Keep every term whose total power of (x−a) and (y−b) is at most n. added for clarity T008 00:06:10

Statements in the class material

“f(x,y)≈f(a,b)+(x−a)fx​(a,b)+(y−b)fy​(a,b)+2!1​[(x−a)2fxx​(a,b)+2(x−a)(y−b)fxy​(a,b)+(y−b)2fyy​(a,b)]” S008·14 · S008·19

Key points

  1. Write the general series first, then find the derivatives, then substitute. That is the order the lecture follows. ESA ★★ T008 00:00:21 · T008 00:02:07
  2. For excosy at (1,4π​) every derivative is ±2​e​; only the signs differ, so take 2​e​ out as a common factor. ESA ★★★ T008 00:04:54 · T008 00:05:19
  3. If the question asks for third degree, you need the third-order partials fxxx​,fxxy​,fxyy​,fyyy​ too. ESA ★★★ T008 00:06:10
  4. Derivatives that vanish at the point drop whole terms: for sin(xy) at (1,2π​) both first partials are 0. ESA ★★ S008·15 · S008·16
  5. Differentiate carefully with the product and chain rules; the slide's own fyy​ for xy2+ycos(x−y) has a sign slip (see the exam traps). ESA ★★ added for clarity S008·17 · S008·18

Exam traps

Exam trap. For xy2+ycos(x−y): fyy​=2x+2sin(x−y)−ycos(x−y), so fyy​(1,1)=1, not 3 as slide 008·18 prints. added for clarity S008·17 · S008·18
Exam trap. Every value on slide 008·3 is ±2​e​, not ±2e​ as said at T008 00:03:47. added for clarity S008·3 · T008 00:03:47

2Maclaurin expansion about the origin

Definitions

Up to three terms. Keep going until there are three non-zero terms: “make sure that you will get three non-zero terms”. S008·5 · T008 00:07:38

Statements in the class material

“Taylor's series about (0,0) is given by f(x,y)≈f(0,0)+fx​(0,0)x+fy​(0,0)y+2!1​[fxx​(0,0)x2+2fxy​(0,0)xy+fyy​(0,0)y2]+3!1​[fxxx​(0,0)x3+3fxxy​(0,0)x2y+3fxyy​(0,0)xy2+fyyy​(0,0)y3]+…” S008·10

Key points

  1. About (0,0) the constant term is often 0 (log1=0, sin0=0), so the series starts at first degree. ESA ★★ T008 00:07:57
  2. “Up to three terms” means three non-zero terms; the lecture still writes all second-order terms. ESA ★★ T008 00:07:38 · T008 00:10:55
  3. Collect and simplify at the end: 2x+y−2x2−2xy−2y2​. ESA ★ T008 00:11:11
  4. Check a two-variable answer with the one-variable series of the combination (E2.2, Figure G2.1). ESA ★★★ added for clarity S008·6 · S008·11

Why?

Why does log(2x+y+1) give (2x+y)−2(2x+y)2​+…?
It is log(1+t) with t=2x+y, and log(1+t)=t−2t2​+3t3​−…; the two-variable Maclaurin series must agree with it.
added for clarityS008·6

Exam traps

Exam trap. For exlog(1+y): fxy​=1+yex​, so fxy​(0,0)=1, not 0 as slide 008·12 lists. added for clarity S008·12
Exam trap. “First six terms” of exlog(1+y) needs third order: y+xy−2y2​+2x2y​−2xy2​+3y3​. The slide stops at three. added for clarity S008·12 · S008·13

3Approximate values from a Taylor polynomial

Definitions

Approximate value. The value of a truncated Taylor polynomial at a point near the expansion point. S008·7 · T008 00:11:47

Key points

  1. Pick an expansion point near the target where f is easy: (1,−1) for (0.9,−1.2), (1,1) for (1.1,0.9). ESA ★★ T008 00:12:10 · CW U1 P26
  2. “The more terms you take, the better the approximation”: the first-order estimate of tan−1(−1.08) is off by 0.012, the second-order one by 0.001. ESA ★★ T008 00:14:18
  3. Substitute h=x−a and k=y−b with their signs: x=0.9 gives h=−0.1, y=−1.2 gives k=−0.2. ESA ★★ T008 00:15:13 · T008 00:15:28

Why?

Why expand about (1, −1) to evaluate at (0.9, −1.2)?
At (1,−1) every value is exact (tan−1(−1)=−4π​, simple derivatives), and h=−0.1, k=−0.2 are small, so the higher terms shrink quickly.
T008 00:12:10 · T008 00:12:48

Exam traps

Exam trap. With x=0.9, h=x−1=−0.1 (negative). A sign slip here changes the answer by 0.1. added for clarity T008 00:15:13
