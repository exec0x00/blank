1Implicit functions, level surfaces and the differential

Definitions

Implicit function. A function given by a relation f(x,y)=c (for example x3y+3xy2=3) rather than by a formula y=g(x). S004·3 · T004a 00:00:18
Level surface (level curve). The set where z=f(x,y)=c (or f(x1​,…,xn​)=c). Examples: equipotential surfaces, isotherms. The cylinders x2+y2=c and the spheres x2+y2+z2=c are layers, one for each c. S004·4 · T004a 00:02:35 · T004a 00:03:29
Total differential. For z=f(x,y): dz=fx​dx+fy​dy. For z=f(x1​,…,xn​): dz=fx1​​dx1​+⋯+fxn​​dxn​. The total derivative is also called the differential. S004·3 · S004·4 · T004a 00:02:04

Statements in the class material

“The total derivative is also called the differential. The differential is used in integration by substitution and in approximation. It is also useful to calculate the Errors in approximation.” S004·4
“If z represents a level surface, i.e. if z=f(x,y)=c, then dz=0. The total derivative is also called the differential.” S004·4

Key points

  1. Integration by substitution uses the differential: with t=x2, dt=2xdx, so ∫ex2xdx=21​∫etdt. ESA ★ transcript only T004a 00:07:57 · T004a 00:08:29
  2. Errors and approximations, another application of the differential, come at the end of the unit. ESA ★ transcript only T004a 00:08:47
  3. Restriction to remember: the formula needs z=f(x,y) to be a constant (a level curve). Move everything to one side first: f(x,y)=c or f(x,y)=0. ESA ★★★ T004a 00:01:49 · T004a 00:02:04
  4. Level surfaces in physics: equipotential surfaces (constant potential) and isotherms (constant temperature). ESA ★ T004a 00:02:35
  5. x2+y2=c gives a family of cylinders (layers), and x2+y2+z2=c1​ a family of spheres about the origin: c1​=0 is the origin, c1​=1 the unit sphere, larger c1​ larger spheres. ESA ★ S004·4 · T004a 00:03:10 · T004a 00:03:42
  6. f(x1​,…,xn​)=c is still a level surface for n>3, even though it cannot be plotted; its total differential is still 0. ESA ★ T004a 00:04:34

2Implicit differentiation in two variables

Statements in the class material

“Setting dxdz​=0 leads to a simple computation formula for implicit differentiation: fx​⋅1+fy​dxdy​=0.” S004·5

Key points

  1. Differentiating an implicit relation directly needs the product rule on every term, and the right-hand constant gives 0. ESA ★★ S004·3 · T004a 00:00:48
  2. The formula dy/dx=−fx​/fy​ gives the same answer and is far easier when f is complicated. ESA ★★ T004a 00:06:19 · T004a 00:06:36
  3. There are two ways to solve “find dy/dx”: differentiate both sides directly, or use −fx​/fy​. Both are accepted. ESA ★★ S003·27 · T003b 00:10:33
  4. The slope dy/dx at a point gives the tangent line y−y0​=m(x−x0​). ESA ★★ S004·7 · T004a 00:12:01
  5. For (cosx)y=(siny)x the result needs cosx>0 and siny>0, because of the logarithms. ESA ★ T004a 00:18:12
  6. Implicit and composite together: if u=tan(x2+y2) and x2−y2=2, use the relation to eliminate y (or find dy/dx) before differentiating. ESA ★★ CW U1 P16 · CW U1 P18

Why?

Why is dy/dx=−fx​/fy​?
Along the curve f(x,y)=c the value of f does not change, so df=fx​dx+fy​dy=0. Divide by dx: fx​+fy​dxdy​=0. The branch diagram (Figure G2.1) shows the same sum.
S004·4 · S004·5 · T004a 00:07:39
Why is the level-curve slope −fx​/fy​ and not −fy​/fx​ (as printed on slide 004·4)?
From fx​+fy​y′=0 you get y′=−fx​/fy​. Test it on x2+y2=5 at (2,1): −fx​/fy​=−2x/(2y)=−2, which matches differentiating directly; −fy​/fx​ would give −1/2.

Exam trap. Slide 004·4 prints dy/dx=−fy​(x,y)/fx​(x,y). The correct formula is dy/dx=−fx​/fy​. added for clarity S004·4
Exam trap. In (cosx)y, with respect to x it is a power (y(cosx)y−1(−sinx)); with respect to y it is an exponential ((cosx)ylncosx). S004·8 · T004a 00:16:02

3Implicit functions of three variables

Definitions

Implicit function of three variables. f(x,y,z)=0 with z (or y) regarded as a function of the remaining variables; the other variables are intermediate variables. S004·6 · T004a 00:09:05

Statements in the class material

“Let f(x,y,z)=0 be the equation of an implicit function of three variables x,y,z. Suppose y and z are functions of x, then f is a function of one independent variable x and y, z are intermediate variables.” S004·6

Key points

  1. For f(x,y,z)=0, x and y are independent, so dz alone has no meaning; find the partials zx​, zy​. ESA ★★ T004a 00:09:38
  2. For ∂z/∂x treat y as constant; for ∂y/∂x treat z as constant. ESA ★★ S004·6 · S004·9 · T004a 00:19:00
  3. The formula avoids separating z from a messy equation such as x3y2+3xyz+sin(ez)=c. ESA ★ T004a 00:10:55
  4. Check that the given point satisfies f=0 before substituting. ESA ★★ added for clarity S004·9 · T004a 00:18:39

Exam traps

Exam trap. The lecture works problem 3 at (1,0,2) and gets 0 for both derivatives, but (1,0,2) is not on z3+xy−y2z=6 (it gives 8). Use the slide's point (0,1,2): ∂y/∂x=1/4, ∂z/∂x=−1/11. added for clarity S004·9 · T004a 00:20:47
Exam trap. Do not differentiate −fx​/fz​ as if z were a constant when you need second derivatives: z is still a function of x and y (W3.3). added for clarity ESA Jan-24 s3 1d

4Composite functions and the chain rule

Definitions

Chain rule (several variables). If z=f(x,y) is a bivariate function and x, y are functions of two or more independent variables u, v, then ∂u∂z​=∂x∂z​∂u∂x​+∂y∂z​∂u∂y​ and ∂v∂z​=∂x∂z​∂v∂x​+∂y∂z​∂v∂y​. S003·13 · T003a 00:21:07
The chain of a problem. The list of dependencies, e.g. “u depends on x,y; x,y depend on r,t; so u is a composite function of r,t”. Knowing it fixes which chain-rule formula to write. T004b 00:02:31

Statements in the class material

“We can define chain rule for a function of two or more independent variable: If z=f(x,y) is a bivariate function and x,y are functions of two or more independent variables say u,v then we can find the partial derivative of z with respect to u and v.” S003·13

Key points

  1. It is essential to know the chain given in the problem: what depends on what. The formula follows from it. ESA ★★★ T004b 00:02:31
  2. When u is a composite function of r,θ (or s,t), eliminate x and y from the final answer: you must know why you substitute. ESA ★★★ T004b 00:07:52 · T004b 00:10:28

Why?

Why does u=f(x−y,y−z,z−x) give ux​+uy​+uz​=0?
Each argument is a difference whose derivatives sum to zero: for r=x−y, rx​+ry​+rz​=1−1+0=0. Adding the three chain-rule expressions gives fr​(0)+fs​(0)+ft​(0). In words: moving x, y, z by the same amount changes none of the arguments.
added for clarityESA Jan-24 s2 1d
Why do the chain-rule answers keep fr​ and fθ​?
The outer function f is unknown, so its partial derivatives cannot be computed; only the inner derivatives (rs​=t, θs​=s/s2+t2​, …) can.

5Composite functions in polar coordinates

Key points

  1. Functions of x2+y2 alone (e.g. sin(x2+y2), ex2+y2) have ∂u/∂θ=0 in polar coordinates, since x2+y2=r2 does not involve θ. ESA ★★ added for clarity S004·10 · S004·13 · S004·14
