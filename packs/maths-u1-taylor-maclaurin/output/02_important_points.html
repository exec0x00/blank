1Why Taylor's and Maclaurin's series?

Key points

  1. Once a function is a polynomial, you can differentiate, integrate, find limits and study how it changes. ESA ★ T007 00:06:50
  2. Applications (one variable): polynomials for complicated functions, definite integrals with no closed form, asymptotic behaviour, growth of functions, differential equations, the Lorentz factor, E=mc2 at low speed, sinθ≈θ for a pendulum, power-flow analysis, generating functions. ESA ★ S007·6 · S007·7 · T007 00:11:31 · T007 00:13:44
  3. Applications (two variables): optimization (a linear or quadratic model), accurate distance measurement with the sine law, variance of complicated configurations, visualizing complicated functions. ESA ★ S007·12 · T007 00:21:22
  4. A few terms are often enough: four terms of the sine series already follow sinx closely near 0, and ten terms overlap it (Figure G1.1). ESA ★ S007·8 · T007 00:15:24 · T007 00:16:10
  5. Taylor's theorem is the basis of L'Hôpital's rule and the mean value theorems of Rolle, Lagrange and Cauchy; the series is a consequence of the theorem. ESA ★ transcript only T007 00:03:33

Why?

Why does a calculator need series at all?
It can only add and multiply. A polynomial needs nothing else, so a function such as etanx is evaluated through polynomial approximations.

2Taylor's and Maclaurin's series in one variable

Definitions

Taylor's series (one variable). If a function f(x) has continuous derivatives of order (n+1), then this function can be expressed as a polynomial at a point x=a in the form f(x)=f(a)+(x−a)f′(a)+2!(x−a)2​f′′(a)+⋯+n!(x−a)n​f(n)(a)+… S007·4 · T007 00:04:19
Maclaurin's series (one variable). If the function is expanded about the origin we get Maclaurin's series: f(x)=f(0)+xf′(0)+2!x2​f′′(0)+⋯+n!xn​f(n)(0)+…; it is a particular case of Taylor's series. S007·4 · T007 00:06:17

Key points

  1. Keep only the first two terms of Taylor's series and you get f(x)≈f(a)+(x−a)f′(a): the tangent line, i.e. the derivative as a slope (Figure G2.1). ESA ★ T007 00:05:26 · T007 00:05:43
  2. To get a standard series, differentiate repeatedly and put x=0: every derivative of ex is ex, which is 1 at the origin. ESA ★ T007 00:09:10 · T007 00:09:44
  3. Substitute into a known series rather than differentiating: sin(x2)=x2−3!x6​+5!x10​−…. ESA ★★ T007 00:11:51
  4. No Maclaurin series exists if f or a derivative is infinite at 0: 1/x, logx, x​. ESA ★ T007 00:10:18

Why?

When is Maclaurin's series valid?
f must have derivatives of every order at 0 (so none is infinite there), and the series must converge to f. ex, sinx, cosx converge for every x; log(1+x) and (1+x)n only for ∣x∣<1.

3Taylor's expansion of a function of two variables

Definitions

Taylor polynomial of degree n. The terms of Taylor's series up to total degree n in (x−a) and (y−b); “expand up to second (third) degree terms” means stop there. Degree 1 is the tangent plane. added for clarity S007·11 · T007 00:20:13

Statements in the class material

“Taylor's series of f(x,y) about (a,b) is given by f(x,y)=f(a,b)+1!1​{(x−a)fx​(a,b)+(y−b)fy​(a,b)}+2!1​{(x−a)2fxx​(a,b)+2(x−a)(y−b)fxy​(a,b)+(y−b)2fyy​(a,b)}+…” S007·9 · T007 00:18:45

Key points

  1. Lay out a table: f, fx​, fy​, fxx​, fxy​, fyy​ (and third order if asked), each in general and then its value at (a,b). Then substitute into E3.1. ESA ★★★ added for clarity CW U1 P24 · ESA Jul-23 1b
  2. There are four second-order partials, but fxy​=fyx​, so they combine into 2(x−a)(y−b)fxy​. ESA ★★ T007 00:18:26
  3. “In powers of (x−1) and (y−4π​)” means about the point (1,4π​). ESA ★★ added for clarity ESA May-24 1b · CW U1 P27
  4. Taylor's series gives approximate values: tan−11.10.9​ from the expansion of tan−1xy​ about (1,1) with h=0.1, k=−0.1. ESA ★★ CW U1 P26
  5. The first-degree Taylor polynomial is the tangent plane; the second degree hugs the surface much better (slide 007·11, Figures G3.1–G3.2). ESA ★ S007·11 · T007 00:20:51

Why?

Why are the second-order coefficients 1, 2, 1?
Put g(t)=f(a+th,b+tk) and use the one-variable Maclaurin series of g at t=0. By the chain rule g′(0)=hfx​+kfy​ and g′′(0)=h2fxx​+2hkfxy​+k2fyy​ (the two equal mixed partials give the 2). Put t=1.

4Maclaurin's expansion of a function of two variables

Definitions

Maclaurin's series (two variables). Taylor's series of f(x,y) about the origin (0,0). S007·10 · T007 00:19:01

Statements in the class material

“Maclaurin's series for a function of two variables is given by f(x,y)=f(0,0)+1!1​{xfx​(0,0)+yfy​(0,0)}+2!1​{x2fxx​(0,0)+2xyfxy​(0,0)+y2fyy​(0,0)}+…” S007·10

Key points

  1. Memory aid from the lecture: fx​ goes with x, fy​ with y, fxx​ with x2, fxy​ with 2xy, fyy​ with y2. ESA ★★ T007 00:19:38
  2. Shortcut and check: if f depends on one combination, use the one-variable series: sin(x+2y)=(x+2y)−6(x+2y)3​+…, log(1+x−y)=(x−y)−2(x−y)2​+3(x−y)3​−… ESA ★★★ added for clarity CW U1 P25 · ESA Jun-24 1c · ESA Jan-24 s1 1b
  3. None of f, fx​, fy​, fxx​, fxy​, fyy​ may be infinite at (0,0). ESA ★ T007 00:19:26
