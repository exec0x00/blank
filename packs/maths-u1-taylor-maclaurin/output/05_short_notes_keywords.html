1Short notes

1 · Why Taylor's and Maclaurin's series?
Series turn complicated functions into polynomials, which can be evaluated, integrated and used in limits: ∫01​sin(x2)dx≈31​−421​+13201​=0.3103; limx3sinx−x​=−61​. Applications: integrals, asymptotics, differential equations, Lorentz factor, sinθ≈θ, optimization, visualization.
2 · Taylor's and Maclaurin's series in one variable
Taylor about a: f(a)+(x−a)f′(a)+2!(x−a)2​f′′(a)+…; Maclaurin about 0. Needs finite derivatives at the point. Standard: ex, log(1+x), (1+x)n, sin, cos, sinh, cosh, tan−1, sin−1, cos−1; log(1+x) and (1+x)n only for ∣x∣<1.
3 · Taylor's expansion of a function of two variables

4Last 10 minutes before the exam

Top facts

  1. Taylor about a: f(a)+(x−a)f′(a)+2!(x−a)2​f′′(a)+…; Maclaurin: about the origin.
  2. Needs finite derivatives at the point; log(1+x), (1+x)n, 1−x1​ only for ∣x∣<1.
  3. Two variables: f+{hfx​+kfy​}+2!1​{h2fxx​+2hkfxy​+k2fyy​}+…, h=x−a, k=y−b.
  4. Third order: 3!1​{h3fxxx​+3h2kfxxy​+3hk2fxyy​+k3fyyy​} (asked in 4 of 6 ESA questions).
  5. Method: derivative table, evaluate at the point, substitute order by order.
  6. One combination (x+2y, x−y): use the one-variable series.
  7. ESA: sinxsiny about (4π​,4π​); excosy about (1,4π​); log(1+x−y); eaxsinby; sin(x+2y).
  8. First degree = tangent plane; more terms follow the surface further.
