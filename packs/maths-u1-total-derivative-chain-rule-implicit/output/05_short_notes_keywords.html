1Short notes

1 · Composite functions and the one-variable chain rule
A composite function is a function of a function, e.g. y=esinx2. Its derivative is found from the outside in and the factors are multiplied: esinx2⋅cosx2⋅2x. For several variables the same idea gives the total derivative and the chain rule.
2 · The total derivative rule
If z=f(x,y) and x=x(t), y=y(t), then z is a function of t alone and its total derivative is dtdz​=∂x∂z​dtdx​+∂y∂z​dtdy​. A tree diagram with one branch per intermediate variable gives the terms. The answer is written in terms of t or evaluated at the given t.
3 · The total differential: approximation and sensitivity
The total differential dz=fx​dx+fy​dy (or ∑fxi​​dxi​) gives the approximate change in z for small changes in the variables. It approximates nearby values, f(x0​+dx,y0​+dy)≈f+fx​dx+fy​dy. It also measures sensitivity: the larger coefficient marks the variable that matters most. It is used in substitution and error estimates.
4 · Rates of change problems
For a rate problem, write the quantity as a function of the changing variables, take d/dt of it with the total derivative rule, and substitute the given values and rates (decreasing means negative). If something stays constant its derivative is 0; a constraint between variables gives a missing rate by implicit differentiation.
5 · The chain rule for composite functions
If z=f(x,y) with x=g(u,v), y=h(u,v), then z is a composite function of u,v and zu​=zx​xu​+zy​yu​, zv​=zx​xv​+zy​yv​ (one term per path in the tree diagram). With three intermediate variables there are three terms. If f is unknown, keep fx​,fy​ as symbols. Write the final answer in the new variables. Standard results: f(x−y,y−z,z−x) gives ux​+uy​+uz​=0; in polar form ux2​+uy2​=ur2​+uθ2​/r2.
6 · Implicit functions of two variables
An implicit function is given by a relation f(x,y)=c, a level curve. Along it df=fx​dx+fy​dy=0, so dxdy​=−fy​fx​​ (fy​=0). Direct differentiation of both sides gives the same result. The slope at (x0​,y0​) gives the tangent y−y0​=m(x−x0​); e.g. xey+yex=0 has tangent x+y=0 at the origin.
7 · Implicit functions of three variables
For f(x,y,z)=0, z is a function of x and y: ∂x∂z​=−fz​fx​​ (hold y), ∂y∂z​=−fz​fy​​, and ∂x∂y​=−fy​fx​​ (hold z), provided the denominators are non-zero. Check that the point lies on the surface. For second derivatives, remember that z still depends on x,y.

4Last 10 minutes before the exam

Top facts

  1. Total derivative: z=f(x,y), x(t), y(t) gives dz/dt=zx​dx/dt+zy​dy/dt (ordinary d).
  2. Chain rule: x(u,v), y(u,v) gives zu​=zx​xu​+zy​yu​, zv​=zx​xv​+zy​yv​ (partial ∂).
  3. Draw the tree diagram and know the chain before writing any formula.
  4. Total differential: dz=fx​dx+fy​dy; it approximates changes and nearby values.
  5. Rates: decreasing means negative; constant means derivative 0; constraints give missing rates.
  6. Implicit, two variables: dy/dx=−fx​/fy​ (slide 004·4 has it upside down).
  7. Implicit, three variables: zx​=−fx​/fz​, zy​=−fy​/fz​, yx​=−fx​/fy​; check that the point is on the surface.
  8. Unknown f: keep fx​, fy​ as symbols; eliminate x,y from final answers in r,θ or s,t.
  9. Exam favourites: ux​+uy​+uz​=0 for f(x−y,y−z,z−x); zx2​+zy2​ identities under u=x2−y2, v=2xy or x=eusinv.
  10. Slide errors to avoid: dz/dt=104t12 (not 60t12), dθ/dt=−0.0481 (not 0.04831), vy​=−3vr​+3vs​.
