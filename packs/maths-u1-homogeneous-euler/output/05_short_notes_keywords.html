1Short notes

1 · Homogeneous functions
u=f(x,y) is homogeneous of degree n if u=xng(y/x) or yng(x/y); in three variables xng(y/x,z/x). Equivalently u(tx,ty)=tnu. A polynomial is homogeneous if all its terms have the same degree. The degree may be positive, negative, fractional or zero, e.g. (x​+y​)/(x+y) has degree −21​.
2 · Euler's theorem
Euler's theorem: if u=f(x,y) is homogeneous of degree n then xux​+yuy​=nu; for f(x,y,z), xux​+yuy​+zuz​=nu. So only homogeneity and the degree are needed, not the derivatives. Proof: differentiate u(tx,ty)=tnu with respect to t and put t=1.
3 · Second and higher order: the corollary
Corollary: x2uxx​+2xyuxy​+y2uyy​=n(n−1)u; adding the first-order result gives n2u. For a sum of homogeneous parts of different degrees apply this to each part, e.g. degrees 3 and 4 give 9u1​+16u2​. Third order: n(n−1)(n−2)u.
4 · Extension: when f(u) is homogeneous
If f(u) (not u) is homogeneous of degree n: xux​+yuy​=g(u)=nf(u)/f′(u) and x2uxx​+2xyuxy​+y2uyy​=g(u)[g′(u)−1]. For u=tan−1xyx4+y4​: f=tan, n=2, g=sin2u, second order sin2u(1−4sin2u).
5 · Applications of homogeneous functions
Constants are homogeneous of degree 0 and linear functions of degree 1. Euler's theorem gives the scaling of a function; it is used in economics (production functions, marginal productivity, elasticity) and in thermodynamics (extensive variables degree 1, intensive degree 0). A function of ratios has degree 0, so its Euler sum is 0.

4Last 10 minutes before the exam

Top facts

  1. Homogeneous of degree n: u=xng(y/x), equivalently u(tx,ty)=tnu.
  2. Degrees add for products and subtract for quotients; any degree is allowed.
  3. Euler: xux​+yuy​=nu (three variables: add zuz​).
  4. Corollary: x2uxx​+2xyuxy​+y2uyy​=n(n−1)u; with the first order added, n2u.
  5. Sums of parts with different degrees: apply each degree to its own part.
  6. Extension: if f(u) has degree n, xux​+yuy​=g=nf/f′ and the second order is g(g′−1).
  7. tan−1(⋅) gives g=2n​sin2u; sin−1(⋅) gives g=ntanu.
  8. Degree 0 means a function of ratios only, so Euler's sum is 0; it does not mean constant.
  9. ESA favourites: 5u & 20u; sin2u & sin2u(1−4sin2u); 9u; 17π/2.
  10. Applications: scaling, economics, thermodynamics (extensive degree 1, intensive degree 0).
