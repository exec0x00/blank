Core explanation · 00:00–04:59

Today's topic will be diode equivalent circuit or diode approximation and series diode configuration.

Key details

  • So coming to the diode approximation, is a mathematical method used to approximate the nonlinear behavior of the real diode to enable the calculations and circuit analysis.
  • There are three different approximations used to analyze the diode circuits, namely the Piecewise linear equivalent circuits,.
  • Simplified diode characteristics, and Ideal diode characteristics.
  • Piecewise linear diode characteristics.
  • Under forward bias, with respect to the V-I characteristics, the diode behaves as a practical voltage source with a magnitude equal to the knee voltage in series with the diode average series with the diode average AC resistance.
  • This is shown the representation.
  • Under reverse bias, the diode is an open switch, and no current flows here through it, act like an ideal diode reverse characteristics.
  • The voltage drop across the diode is calculated by the formula VD is equal to Vb plus ID into RB.
  • Coming to the Simplified diode characteristics, under forward bias, the diode turns on when a voltage VK or VB is applied across it.
  • It turns off if the voltage applied is less than the VK.
  • Thus, diode is represented by voltage of VK in a series with a closed path switch, as shown.
  • Therefore, VD is equal to VB.
  • Then coming to the reverse bias, it is an open switch similar to that of a practical reverse bias voltage.
  • Coming to the Ideal diode characteristics here, we already know the diode is considered as a closed switch with a zero voltage drop.
  • Then when in forward bias VD and open switch when it's in reverse bias.
  • When the diode is connected in series, we call it as a diode configuration, a series diode configuration with a resistor.
  • It is assumed that the forward characteristics or forward resistance of the diode is usually so small compared to the other series elements of the network that can be ignored.
  • In general, the diode is in on state if the current established by the applied sources is such that its direction matches that of an arrow in the diode symbol and applied voltage across the diode is silicon VD greater than 0.7, germanium VD greater than 0.3, and gallium arsenide VD should be greater than 1.2 volts.
  • And when it's reverse bias, diode is reverse connected to the positive voltage here and represented as an open circuit, and hence ID is current is zero here, and VR also is equal to zero volts.
  • This is with respect to series diode configuration with a resistor.
  • Assume the LED voltage as 0.7.
  • We have a silicon and a LED here, and we have an output resistance 680 ohm.
  • Then we need to apply the KVL here.
  • We need to remember the KVL equation, which we learned from 12th standard.
  • The sum of all the voltages across the closed loop network is always equal to zero.
