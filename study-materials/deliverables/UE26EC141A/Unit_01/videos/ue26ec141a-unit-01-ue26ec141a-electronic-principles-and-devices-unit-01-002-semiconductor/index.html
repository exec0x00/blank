Core explanation · 00:00–04:59

Today we will go with the topic three, Introduction to Semiconductor.

Key details

  • In the last class we have looked at the diode and biasing of the diode, where we know that diode has two regions, P and N type, where P side is connected with the biasing.
  • P side is connected to the positive of the terminal and N side is connected to negative of the battery terminal.
  • P and N region not connected to the voltage supply.
  • We call it as a no bias.
  • That means to say, with no voltage applied to the diode, we see that the boundary, there is a depletion region with the charges being accumulated.
  • This happens because at the instant the two metals are joined, the electrons and the holes in the region of the junction will combine, resulting in lack of free charges in the region near the junction, as shown, which we call it as a depletion region.
  • And note in the figure that only particle displayed in this region are positive and negative ion remaining once the free charges have been observed.
  • That means to say, because of the concentration gradient, the charge carriers will start move from.
  • P side to N side and N side to P side.
  • As the moment they start moving, they forms a charges which is been settled at the boundaries of the P and N, where negative charge is settled at the P region boundary and positive charge is settled at the N region boundary.
  • This region of uncovered positive and negative ion is called depletion region.
  • This is due to the depletion of free charge carrier in the region.
  • So which we call it as a no bias condition.
  • As the name indicates, a forward bias or 'ON' condition.
  • The current that exists under the forward bias is called the forward bias current and is represented by ID in the diagram.
  • As the name indicates, forward bias, we connect P region to positive terminal of the battery and N region to negative terminal of the battery.
  • As the moment we connect it, we call it as an 'ON' condition, that is established by applying a positive potential to the P terminal and a negative potential to the N terminal, as shown in the diagram.
  • The application of the forward bias potential that is VD we call here, will pressurize or will pressure the electron in the N-type material and hole in the.
  • P-type material to recombine with ion near the boundary and reduces the width of the depletion region, as shown here.
  • If compared to the previous diagram, we have a very big depletion region which is shown here.
  • And because of the application of the voltage, the depletion region is reduced and we see only a few charge carrier.
  • This happens due to the application of the voltage on both P and N region, and which results in the ID current which is a sum of the eye majority and the leakage current.
  • This can be represented by a V-A characteristic, as shown, the forward bias current is shown here.
  • There is a very less resistance is there in the forward bias and we see that there is a current flow with application of the voltage.
  • This is about the forward bias.
  • Then what is reverse bias?
  • The reverse bias condition, the P side is connected to the negative terminal of the battery, N side is connected to the positive terminal of the battery with applied voltage VD.
  • It's increasing due to the large number of free electron drawn from the positive terminal of the applied voltage.
  • For a similar reason, the number of uncovered ion will increase in the P-type material.
  • The net effect therefore is widening of a depletion region, as shown.
